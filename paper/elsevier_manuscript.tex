\title{Steering Through the Trees: Physical Lie Vector Field Evasion of Tree Ensembles in Network Intrusion Detection}

\author[inst1]{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
Gradient-boosted trees dominate flow-based network intrusion detection, yet their worst-case robustness to schema-feasible evasion is rarely measured. Flow-Field Evasion (FFE) models packet-level actions as commuting generators on the flow manifold. After diagnosing FFE-v1, we introduce FFE-v2 (re-segmentation, multi-scale probing, data-driven window targets), which uses scores only. On three full, deduplicated corpora and three libraries, it matches or exceeds our greedy threshold-crossing control and FFE-v1 in all nine cells (significantly above the control in four); on XGBoost its Real ASR is 99.5\%, 100.0\% and 98.3\%, against 57.6\% for FFE-v1 and 21.1\% for CAPGD on UNSW-NB15. It needs 28--38 queries (control: 2). CatBoost on CIC-IDS2017 (36.4\%) is a ceiling set by immutable features. Feasibility is relative to a schema verifier, and chaff accounting matters on UNSW-NB15. As a defense, a monotone gate is OR-fused with a free classifier (hardened on five of nine cells). If backward features stay fixed, flagged flows stay flagged. Against FFE-v1, CAPGD and a greedy white-box attacker, Real ASR is at most 8.8\% on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CIC-IDS2017 and 1.0--32\% on UNSW-NB15, one seed; FFE-v2 was not run against it. The single test evaluation is not a clean hold-out; five-seed runs are cleaner.
\end{abstract}
