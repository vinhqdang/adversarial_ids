\title{Steering Through the Trees: Physical Lie Vector Field Evasion of Tree Ensembles in Network Intrusion Detection}

\author[inst1]{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
Gradient-boosted decision trees dominate flow-based network intrusion detection, yet their robustness to physically realizable evasion is rarely measured at scale. Feature-space attacks ignore the coupling invariants of flow records and produce telemetry that no packet sequence can generate, while stochastic packet mutators need hundreds of queries. We propose Flow-Field Evasion (FFE), which models four protocol-compliant actions (temporal dilation, payload padding, chaff injection, TCP window morphing) as infinitesimal generators of a Lie cone on the flow manifold, steers along the cheapest generator, and snaps to tree split thresholds. On the complete, deduplicated CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (4.3 million flows), CIC-IDS2017 (1.7 million) and UNSW-NB15 (120 thousand) captures, FFE evades XGBoost in 100.0\%, 95.9\% and 61.5\% of detected attacks, all physically feasible, with 13--91 queries. It outperforms Traffic Manipulator PSO and CAA/MOEVA on every corpus; against surrogate-based CAPGD it is significantly better on two corpora and statistically tied on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (100.0\% vs. 99.5\%), where CAPGD needs fewer queries. Lie-guided adversarial training lowers FFE success from 96.3\% to 33.0\% without clean-accuracy loss, whereas malleability penalties alone reach 67.0\%.
\end{abstract}

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Operational Feasibility, Query Efficiency, and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Feature-space attacks are fast, but their output is not guaranteed to be a physically realizable flow (Section~\ref{sec:sota}). FFE pays for feasibility with a different structure. Its generators act on the flow manifold, so trajectories in the continuous Lie cone satisfy the conservation and rate-coupling invariants exactly, and a projection step restores them on the discrete flow schema. All evasive flows that FFE produced passed the feasibility check (100.0\% PFR on every corpus and architecture). This figure measures conformance to the 12-feature schema under discrete projection, not an independent test on live packets.

The comparison with the baselines is not a single ranking. Against the constraint-respecting query-based methods (PSO and CAA/MOEVA), FFE is better on every corpus (for example 61.5\% against 43.5\% for PSO on UNSW-NB15) with 2.5--19 times fewer queries, because TSBS snaps flows onto adjacent split boundaries instead of searching by stochastic dispersion. Against surrogate-based attacks it depends on the corpus. CAPGD reaches 99.5\% on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 with 1.1 queries per flow, statistically indistinguishable from FFE's 100.0\% at 13.0 queries, but falls to 55.2\% and 26.8\% on CIC-IDS2017 and UNSW-NB15, where FFE reaches 95.9\% and 61.5\%. An attacker with a good surrogate can therefore do very well on some corpora, and FFE's advantage is largest where the surrogate imitates the target poorly. Its cost advantage (0.13--0.92 operational units against 30 to $2\times10^{7}$) is partly by construction, since FFE steers by the cost model that defines the column.

The method has clear limits. TSBS requires Tier-1 white-box access to split thresholds. Under Tier-2 access the attack depends on probes and transfer, and flows crafted against one architecture evade other architectures in 34\%--83\% of the cases on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (Supplementary Section~S5), with CatBoost the hardest target. Traffic Manipulator operates on captured PCAP files, whereas our packet realization is a Scapy/NFQueue proxy that has not been replayed at line rate. Control is restricted to forward traffic, backward server features are immutable, deep packet inspection is out of scope, and rate dilution lowers the intensity of volumetric floods.

Evasion efficacy depends on the corpus and the architecture. XGBoost on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 places the root split of 55 of its 60 trees on destination port, an immutable feature, and is nevertheless evaded in every attacked flow, so immutability of the root feature does not by itself protect a model. On CIC-IDS2017 XGBoost is evaded in 95.9\% of the cohort and LightGBM in 34.6\%; we offer no mechanism for this difference. UNSW-NB15 is the hardest corpus for all attacks, although its lossy 12-feature mapping also makes the detector weaker (88.8\% accuracy).

Threats to validity are as follows. Each experiment uses one training seed, so the intervals reflect cohort sampling and not retraining variance. Cohorts contain only flows that the target detects. CAA/MOEVA and PSO ran on 200 flows, the ablations and defenses on 300, the others on 1,000. CAPGD and the NIDSGAN-style generator are our re-implementations with one small surrogate and one hyperparameter setting, so a better-tuned surrogate attack could do better. ``Full scale'' refers to the distributed processed files (CSE-CIC-IDS2018 files are capped at 1,048,575 rows; the UNSW-NB15 files are the official partition). Deduplication removes 19\%--44\% of the valid records, so a detector trained on raw, duplicated traffic would see a different distribution, and the 12-feature schema makes accuracies incomparable with all-feature studies. The defenses were evaluated only against the attack that produced their augmentation and only on CSE-CIC-IDS2018.

Each generator has a protocol-compliant packet realization: sender pacing for temporal dilation, padding in protocol fields or ignored application frames (\texttt{SSH\_MSG\_IGNORE}) \cite{rfc4253}, payload-free pure ACK or keep-alive chaff \cite{rfc9293,rfc7323} or zero-length UDP datagrams \cite{rfc768}, and handshake window morphing, with sequence numbers and checksums updated via proxy \cite{rfc791,rfc9293,rfc768}.

\subsection{Concluding Remarks and Future Directions}
\label{sec:concluding_remarks}

This article has measured, at full scale, how vulnerable tree-ensemble NIDS with high benchmark accuracy are to evasion that respects flow physics. Flow-Field Evasion models four packet-level actions as infinitesimal generators on the flow manifold; trajectories in the continuous Lie cone preserve the conservation invariants and a projection enforces them on the discrete schema. On the complete, deduplicated CSE-CIC-IDS2018, CIC-IDS2017 and UNSW-NB15 captures, FFE evades XGBoost in 100.0\%, 95.9\% and 61.5\% of detected attacks with 100.0\% physical feasibility and 13.0--91.4 queries per flow. It is significantly more successful and cheaper in queries than the constraint-respecting black-box baselines, significantly more successful than surrogate-based CAPGD on two corpora, and statistically tied with it on the third, where CAPGD needs fewer queries. Evasion is not a property of the attack alone: LightGBM on CIC-IDS2017 resists it far better than XGBoost, and CatBoost resists transfer from the other ensembles. Lie-guided adversarial training reduces FFE success from 96.3\% to 33.0\% without affecting clean accuracy, whereas masking malleable features helps less (67.0\%) and adds nothing on top of adversarial training. Future work includes multi-seed evaluation, defenses that withstand attacks other than the one used for augmentation, the remaining public NIDS corpora, line-rate replay on a hardware testbed, and certified robustness bounds for adversarially trained ensembles.
