\title{Steering Through the Trees: Physical Lie Vector Field Evasion of Tree Ensembles in Network Intrusion Detection}

\author[inst1]{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}
\ead{vinh.dq4@buv.edu.vn}
\affiliation[inst1]{organization={School of Innovative and Computing Sciences, British University Vietnam},
            city={Hung Yen},
            country={Vietnam}}

\begin{abstract}
Gradient-boosted decision trees dominate flow-based network intrusion detection, yet their robustness to schema-feasible evasion is rarely measured at scale. Feature-space attacks ignore the coupling invariants of flow records, while stochastic packet mutators need hundreds of queries. We propose Flow-Field Evasion (FFE), which models four protocol-compliant actions (temporal dilation, payload padding, chaff injection, TCP window morphing) as infinitesimal generators of a Lie cone on the flow manifold, steers along the cheapest generator and finishes with a snap to tree split thresholds. On the complete, deduplicated CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (5.0 million flows), CIC-IDS2017 (1.7 million) and UNSW-NB15 (124 thousand) captures, FFE evades XGBoost in 99.5\%, 99.2\% and 57.6\% of the detected attacks it targets, all schema-feasible, with 15--94 target queries. It is significantly more successful than seven baselines, including surrogate-based CAPGD (88.5\%, 89.5\%, 21.1\%), and stays ahead over five retrained seeds, although CAPGD needs far fewer queries and boundary snapping adds at most three points. Lie-guided adversarial training halves FFE success at the budget it was trained on (84.1\% to 46.5\%) but protects much less against a larger budget (80.3\%); no defense we tested brings evasion below 46\%.
\end{abstract}
