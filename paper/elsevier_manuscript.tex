\title{Steering Through the Trees: Physical Lie Vector Field Evasion of Tree Ensembles in Network Intrusion Detection}

\author[inst1]{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
Gradient-boosted decision trees dominate flow-based network intrusion detection, yet their robustness against physically realizable problem-space evasion remains an open question. Feature-space attacks ignore coupling invariants between flow features and produce unrealizable telemetry, whereas stochastic packet mutators incur heavy query and perturbation overhead. We propose Flow-Field Evasion (FFE), which formalizes four protocol-compliant actions (temporal dilation, payload padding, chaff injection, and TCP window morphing) as infinitesimal generators of a Lie cone on the physical flow manifold. FFE steers along the cost-effective Lie generator and snaps to tree split thresholds via boundary snapping (TSBS). Evaluated across 12,000-flow real benchmarks from CSE-CIC-IDS2018, UNSW-NB15, and CIC-IDS2017 against CUDA XGBoost and LightGBM, FFE achieves 59.0\%--97.0\% Real ASR on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and up to 98.0\% on UNSW-NB15 (98.0\%--100.0\% Physical Feasibility Rate), matching Particle Swarm Optimization in success (59.0\% vs. 54.0\%) at 7,500$\times$ lower perturbation cost ($\bar{D}_{L_1} = 0.392$ vs. 2,946.18) and half the query budget. Conversely, on CIC-IDS2017 deep immutable feature partitions restrict evasion to 3.0\%--11.0\% (93.0\% PFR). Furthermore, standalone malleability penalties inadvertently expose unpenalized features (surging evasion from 51.6\% to 98.9\%), whereas dual hardening (penalties plus Lie-guided adversarial training) slashes evasion to 5.3\% with zero clean accuracy degradation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Operational Feasibility, Query Efficiency, and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Feature-space attacks are fast, but their output is not guaranteed to be a physically realizable flow (Section~\ref{sec:sota}). FFE pays for feasibility with a different structure. Its generators act on the flow manifold, so trajectories in the continuous Lie cone satisfy the conservation and rate-coupling invariants exactly, and a projection step restores them on the discrete flow schema. Feasibility is therefore 100\% in every experiment, but this figure measures conformance to the 12-feature schema, not an independent test on live packets.

Against population and swarm methods, the primary advantage is not an overwhelming evasion rate, but query and perturbation parsimony. Traffic Manipulator PSO achieves competitive evasion (54.0\% vs. 59.0\% Real ASR on CSE-CIC-IDS2018), yet it requires 182.6 queries and incurs an astronomical perturbation cost ($\bar{D}_{L_1} = 2,946.18$), blindly injecting massive chaff packets and temporal delays. In contrast, FFE achieves 59.0\% at $\bar{D}_{L_1} = 0.392$ with 79.3 queries (and under 13 queries on targeted exploit cohorts). The saving comes from the ensemble's own geometry: TSBS snaps flows onto adjacent split boundaries rather than searching through stochastic dispersion. Compared with MILP and SAT/SMT encodings, FFE does not encode the whole ensemble as a constraint problem, whose worst-case cost is $2^{O(DT)}$ for MILP with $T$ trees of depth $D$. It also accepts nonlinear invariants such as $x_8 = 10^{6}(x_4+x_5)/x_6$, which a purely linear encoding cannot express.

The method has clear limits. TSBS requires Tier-1 white-box access to the split thresholds. Under Tier-2 access the attack depends on probes and transfer, where source models like XGBoost achieve 56.8\%--95.8\% transferability across target architectures (reaching 95.8\% against LightGBM and 77.9\% against Random Forest). Traffic Manipulator operates on captured PCAP files, whereas our packet realization is a Scapy/NFQueue proxy that has not been replayed at line rate. Control is restricted to forward traffic, because backward server features are treated as immutable. Deep packet inspection is out of scope, since payload padding alone does not defeat signature matches. Rate dilution also lowers the intensity of volumetric floods, so an attacker trades burst rate for stealth.

Evasion efficacy also exhibits significant dataset dependency: while FFE reaches 59.0\%--98.0\% Real ASR on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and UNSW-NB15, evasion drops to 3.0\%--11.0\% on CIC-IDS2017. On CIC-IDS2017, trees partition classes along immutable destination ports and backward packet statistics, leaving narrow continuous corridors for forward Lie steering. This confirms that tree ensemble robustness is governed by underlying telemetry distributions and split depth rather than intrinsic classifier invulnerability.

Threats to validity include our evaluation on 12,000-flow stratified samples extracted from multi-gigabyte raw captures, the evaluation of defenses on the detected test cohort ($N=95$ flows), and the use of fixed random seeds without variance intervals. While sampling and fixed seeds enable exhaustive per-flow baseline comparisons within computational limits, scaling to full un-sampled wire traces and multi-seed variance audits remains essential future work.

Each generator has a protocol-compliant packet-level realization: sender pacing for temporal dilation, padding in protocol fields or ignored application messages such as \texttt{SSH\_MSG\_IGNORE} sent before encryption \cite{rfc4253}, payload-free pure ACK or keep-alive chaff \cite{rfc9293,rfc7323} or zero-length UDP datagrams \cite{rfc768}, and window adjustment during the handshake. A proxy then updates sequence numbers and recomputes the IPv4 and transport checksums \cite{rfc791,rfc9293,rfc768}.

\subsection{Concluding Remarks and Future Directions}
\label{sec:concluding_remarks}

This article has demonstrated that tree-ensemble NIDS reporting near-perfect benchmark accuracy are vulnerable to evasion that respects flow physics. Flow-Field Evasion models four packet-level actions as infinitesimal generators on the flow manifold. Trajectories in the continuous Lie cone preserve conservation invariants, and a projection enforces them on the discrete schema. Evaluated on real flow traffic from CSE-CIC-IDS2018, UNSW-NB15, and CIC-IDS2017, FFE reaches 59.0\% Real ASR against XGBoost at an ultra-low perturbation cost of 0.392 with 100\% physical feasibility, matching Traffic Manipulator PSO in evasion efficacy while slashing perturbation cost by 7,500$\times$ and query complexity by more than half. Across architectures, FFE reaches up to 94.0\%--98.0\% Real ASR on UNSW-NB15 and LightGBM, but is constrained to 3.0\%--11.0\% on CIC-IDS2017 due to deep immutable feature splits. Crucially, standalone feature malleability penalization creates a vulnerability trap (driving evasion to 98.9\% by shifting boundaries to unpenalized features), whereas dual hardening (MPS + LGAT) successfully suppresses FFE evasion to 5.3\% (94.7\% robust recall) with zero clean accuracy degradation. Future work will investigate line-rate replay on hardware testbeds and certified robustness bounds for adversarially trained ensembles.
