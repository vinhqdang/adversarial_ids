\title{Steering Through the Trees: Physical Lie Vector Field Evasion of Tree Ensembles in Network Intrusion Detection}

\author[inst1]{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
Gradient-boosted tree ensembles dominate flow-based network intrusion detection, yet their robustness to evasion that respects flow semantics is unclear. Feature-space attacks ignore the coupling between flow features and yield unrealizable flows, whereas packet-level heuristics treat the detector as a black box and need thousands of queries. We propose Flow-Field Evasion (FFE), which models four attacker-controlled actions (temporal dilation, payload padding, chaff injection and TCP window morphing) as generators of a Lie cone on the flow manifold. We prove that trajectories in the continuous relaxation of this cone preserve the rate, length, header and MTU invariants, and a projection enforces them on the discrete flow schema. FFE follows the generator with the largest cost-weighted score decrease, then snaps to tree-split boundaries. Against CUDA XGBoost on a corpus calibrated to CSE-CIC-IDS2018, FFE reaches 100\% real attack success with 12.9 queries per flow, versus 48.0\% and 82.0\% for CAA/MOEVA and PSO at 1,240 and 1,850 queries. On corpora calibrated to UNSW-NB15 and CIDDS-001 and against LightGBM, it stays at 100\% with 6.8--17.8 queries. Feature masking with single-round adversarial training lowers success from 95.0\% to 56.0\% against a non-adaptive attacker (five-step budget), at a 2.42-point accuracy cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Feature-space attacks are fast, but their output is not guaranteed to be a physically realizable flow (Section~\ref{sec:sota}). FFE pays for feasibility with a different structure. Its generators act on the flow manifold, so trajectories in the continuous Lie cone satisfy the conservation and rate-coupling invariants exactly, and a projection step restores them on the discrete flow schema. Feasibility is therefore 100\% in every experiment, but this figure measures conformance to the 12-feature schema, not an independent test on live packets.

Against population and swarm methods, the advantage is query efficiency. CAA/MOEVA and Traffic Manipulator PSO need 1,240 and 1,850 queries, against 12.9 for FFE on the primary benchmark. The saving comes from the ensemble's own geometry: TSBS snaps flows onto split boundaries instead of searching blindly. Compared with MILP and SAT/SMT encodings, FFE does not encode the whole ensemble as a constraint problem, whose worst-case cost is $2^{O(DT)}$ for MILP with $T$ trees of depth $D$. It also accepts nonlinear invariants such as $x_8 = 10^{6}(x_4+x_5)/x_6$, which a purely linear encoding cannot express.

The method has clear limits. TSBS requires Tier-1 white-box access to the split thresholds. Under Tier-2 access the attack depends on probes and transfer, and transferability is high but not complete, with off-diagonal rates of 69--94\%. Traffic Manipulator operates on captured PCAP files, whereas our packet realization is a Scapy/NFQueue proxy that has not been replayed at line rate. Control is restricted to forward traffic, because backward server features are treated as immutable. Deep packet inspection is out of scope, since payload padding alone does not defeat signature matches. Rate dilution also lowers the intensity of volumetric floods, so an attacker trades burst rate for stealth. The corpora are synthetic and calibrated to published marginal distributions rather than replayed captures, and the strongest defense we tested still leaves 56.0\% of FFE attacks successful.

Each generator has a protocol-compliant packet-level realization: sender pacing for temporal dilation, padding in protocol fields or ignored application messages such as \texttt{SSH\_MSG\_IGNORE} sent before encryption \cite{rfc4253}, payload-free pure ACK or keep-alive chaff \cite{rfc9293,rfc7323} or zero-length UDP datagrams \cite{rfc768}, and window adjustment during the handshake. A proxy then updates sequence numbers and recomputes the IPv4 and transport checksums \cite{rfc791,rfc9293,rfc768}.

We ran all experiments on synthetic benchmarks in a controlled setting. The aim is to expose a weakness of GBDT detectors before it is exploited, and the results support the robust training of Section~\ref{sec:defense}.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This article has shown that tree-ensemble NIDS with near-perfect benchmark accuracy are vulnerable to evasion that respects flow physics. Flow-Field Evasion models four packet-level actions as infinitesimal generators on the flow manifold. Trajectories in the continuous Lie cone preserve the conservation invariants, and a projection enforces them on the discrete schema. FFE reaches 100\% real attack success against XGBoost at 12.9 queries per flow, and 100\% on three corpora and two gradient-boosting libraries at 6.8--17.8 queries per flow, whereas baseline real attack success ranges from 0.0\% to 82.0\% in our comparison. Feature masking combined with single-round FFE adversarial training reduces FFE success from 95.0\% to 56.0\% at a five-step budget against a non-adaptive attacker, at a 2.42-point clean-accuracy cost. This is a partial defense, and adaptive attackers were not evaluated. Future work will address line-rate replay on hardware and certified robustness bounds for adversarially trained ensembles.
