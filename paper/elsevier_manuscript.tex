\title{Steering Through the Trees: Physical Lie Vector Field Evasion of Tree Ensembles in Network Intrusion Detection}

\author[inst1]{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
Gradient-boosted tree ensembles dominate flow-based network intrusion detection, yet their robustness against physically realizable problem-space evasion remains an open question. Feature-space attacks ignore coupling invariants between flow features and produce unrealizable telemetry, whereas black-box packet heuristics require high query budgets. We propose Flow-Field Evasion (FFE), which formalizes four protocol-compliant actions (temporal dilation, payload padding, chaff injection, and TCP window morphing) as infinitesimal generators of a Lie cone on the physical flow manifold. We prove that continuous trajectories within this cone preserve rate, header, and MTU invariants, while an invariant projection restores schema conformance. FFE greedily steers along the most cost-effective Lie generator and snaps to tree split thresholds via threshold-snapping binary search (TSBS). Evaluated on real flow traffic from CSE-CIC-IDS2018, UNSW-NB15, and CIC-IDS2017 against CUDA XGBoost and LightGBM, FFE achieves 59.0\% Real ASR with 100\% Physical Feasibility Rate and an ultra-low perturbation cost of $\bar{D}_{L_1} = 0.392$, outperforming Particle Swarm Optimization (54.0\% Real ASR at 7,500$\times$ higher cost) while unconstrained baselines (HopSkipJump, ZOO, MOEVA) collapse to 0\%--1\% Real ASR. Finally, dual hardening via malleability penalties and Lie-guided adversarial training slashes FFE evasion from 51.6\% to 5.3\% with zero clean accuracy loss.
\end{abstract}

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Operational Feasibility, Query Efficiency, and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Feature-space attacks are fast, but their output is not guaranteed to be a physically realizable flow (Section~\ref{sec:sota}). FFE pays for feasibility with a different structure. Its generators act on the flow manifold, so trajectories in the continuous Lie cone satisfy the conservation and rate-coupling invariants exactly, and a projection step restores them on the discrete flow schema. Feasibility is therefore 100\% in every experiment, but this figure measures conformance to the 12-feature schema, not an independent test on live packets.

Against population and swarm methods, the advantage is query efficiency. CAA/MOEVA and Traffic Manipulator PSO require 336.2 and 182.6 queries per flow, against 79.3 for FFE on the primary benchmark (and under 13 queries on targeted exploit cohorts). The saving comes from the ensemble's own geometry: TSBS snaps flows onto split boundaries instead of searching blindly. Compared with MILP and SAT/SMT encodings, FFE does not encode the whole ensemble as a constraint problem, whose worst-case cost is $2^{O(DT)}$ for MILP with $T$ trees of depth $D$. It also accepts nonlinear invariants such as $x_8 = 10^{6}(x_4+x_5)/x_6$, which a purely linear encoding cannot express.

The method has clear limits. TSBS requires Tier-1 white-box access to the split thresholds. Under Tier-2 access the attack depends on probes and transfer, where source models like XGBoost achieve 56.8\%--95.8\% transferability across target architectures (reaching 95.8\% against LightGBM and 77.9\% against Random Forest). Traffic Manipulator operates on captured PCAP files, whereas our packet realization is a Scapy/NFQueue proxy that has not been replayed at line rate. Control is restricted to forward traffic, because backward server features are treated as immutable. Deep packet inspection is out of scope, since payload padding alone does not defeat signature matches. Rate dilution also lowers the intensity of volumetric floods, so an attacker trades burst rate for stealth. Our evaluations are conducted on real benchmark datasets (CSE-CIC-IDS2018, UNSW-NB15, and CIC-IDS2017) mapped to flow telemetry; while real network traffic was evaluated, live packet replay across physical switches remains future work, and the strongest defense we tested (dual MPS + LGAT) successfully curtails FFE evasion to 5.3\% (94.7\% robust recall).

Each generator has a protocol-compliant packet-level realization: sender pacing for temporal dilation, padding in protocol fields or ignored application messages such as \texttt{SSH\_MSG\_IGNORE} sent before encryption \cite{rfc4253}, payload-free pure ACK or keep-alive chaff \cite{rfc9293,rfc7323} or zero-length UDP datagrams \cite{rfc768}, and window adjustment during the handshake. A proxy then updates sequence numbers and recomputes the IPv4 and transport checksums \cite{rfc791,rfc9293,rfc768}.

We ran all experiments on benchmark network datasets in a controlled setting. The aim is to expose a weakness of GBDT detectors before it is exploited, and the results support the robust training of Section~\ref{sec:defense}.

\subsection{Concluding Remarks and Future Directions}
\label{sec:concluding_remarks}

This article has shown that tree-ensemble NIDS with near-perfect benchmark accuracy are vulnerable to evasion that respects flow physics. Flow-Field Evasion models four packet-level actions as infinitesimal generators on the flow manifold. Trajectories in the continuous Lie cone preserve conservation invariants, and a projection enforces them on the discrete schema. Evaluated on real flow traffic from CSE-CIC-IDS2018, UNSW-NB15, and CIC-IDS2017, FFE reaches 59.0\% Real ASR against XGBoost at an ultra-low perturbation cost of 0.392 with 100\% physical feasibility, outperforming Traffic Manipulator PSO (54.0\% Real ASR at 7,500$\times$ higher cost) while mathematical baselines collapse to 0\%--1\% Real ASR. On UNSW-NB15 and LightGBM, FFE reaches 94.0\%--98.0\% Real ASR. Finally, dual hardening via malleability-penalized splitting and Lie-guided adversarial training (MPS + LGAT) reduces FFE evasion from 51.6\% to 5.3\% (raising robust recall to 94.7\%) with zero clean accuracy penalty (98.67\% accuracy and 99.84\% AUC). Future work will address line-rate replay on hardware testbeds and certified robustness bounds for adversarially trained ensembles.
