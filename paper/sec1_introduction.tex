\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) increasingly rely on machine learning over flow-based telemetry. At multi-gigabit rates, inspecting every packet payload is prohibitive, so sensors such as CICFlowMeter \cite{sharafaldin2018toward} aggregate bidirectional packet exchanges into tabular flow records of timing, length, header and flag statistics. Gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs), notably XGBoost \cite{chen2016xgboost}, LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} and CatBoost \cite{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, are the dominant classifiers for these records.

Near-perfect accuracy on benchmark corpora does not guarantee robustness in operation. Closed-world evaluations reward models that exploit testbed artifacts \cite{sommer2010outside} and shortcut features \cite{geirhos2020shortcut}. Attackers, however, control the malleable parts of their traffic, such as timing, padding and window sizes, so a detector that relies on these features can be evaded without losing the exploit \cite{apruzzese2022modeling}.

Evading a tree ensemble is hard for two reasons. First, the ensemble is piecewise constant, so $\nabla_x F(x)=0$ for almost all $x$ and gradient-based attacks receive no signal. Second, flow features obey conservation laws. Flow Bytes/s satisfies $x_8 = 10^6(x_4+x_5)/x_6$, where $x_4$ and $x_5$ are the forward and backward byte totals and $x_6$ is the duration in microseconds. A perturbation that changes the byte totals without updating the rate produces a phantom flow that protocol sanity checks reject \cite{pierazzi2020intrusion, apruzzese2022modeling}.

Existing work follows one of two paradigms, and neither is adequate. Feature-space methods such as ZOO \cite{chen2017zoo} and HopSkipJump \cite{chen2020hopskipjump} ignore the physical coupling: when evaluated against network protocol invariants, their Physical Feasibility Rate (PFR) collapses to 0.0\%--6.0\%, yielding 0.0\% Real ASR despite reporting up to 100\% mathematical evasion in unconstrained coordinates. Heuristic multi-objective search (CAA/MOEVA) \cite{simonetto2024constrained, ghamizi2020moeva} achieves only 1.0\% Real ASR (15.0\% PFR) after 336.2 queries per flow. Conversely, packet-level trial-and-error tools such as Traffic Manipulator PSO \cite{han2021traffic} preserve physical feasibility (100.0\% PFR), but query the classifier blindly as a black box, requiring 182.6 queries per flow and achieving 54.0\% Real ASR at severe perturbation overhead ($\bar{D}_{L_1} = 2,946.18$).

This article proposes Flow-Field Evasion (FFE), which combines both properties. FFE derives physical perturbations as differential generators, so the continuous flow satisfies the invariants exactly and a projection enforces them on the discrete schema, and it steers flows using the split geometry of the ensemble. To our knowledge, no prior attack on tree ensembles combines these two properties.

The main contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item We model four packet-level attacker actions (temporal dilation, payload padding, chaff injection and TCP window morphing) as closed-form infinitesimal generators of a Lie cone on the flow manifold.
    \item We prove that trajectories in this cone preserve the rate, mean-length, header and MTU invariants of flow records in the continuous relaxation of the flow manifold.
    \item We present FFE, which moves each flow along the generator with the largest cost-weighted score decrease and finishes with tree-split boundary snapping (TSBS).
    \item We evaluate FFE on real benchmark traffic from CSE-CIC-IDS2018, UNSW-NB15, and CIC-IDS2017. Against CUDA XGBoost on CSE-CIC-IDS2018, FFE achieves 59.0\% Real ASR (100.0\% PFR), achieving comparable nominal evasion to Traffic Manipulator PSO (59.0\% vs. 54.0\%, within overlapping confidence intervals) while cutting perturbation overhead by $7,500\times$ ($\bar{D}_{L_1} = 0.392$ vs. 2,946.18) and queries by $2.3\times$ (79.3 vs. 182.6). Across architectures and datasets, FFE reaches up to 94.0\%--98.0\% Real ASR on UNSW-NB15 and LightGBM (93.0\%--100.0\% PFR overall), but is constrained to 3.0\%--11.0\% on CIC-IDS2017 due to root splits on immutable backward traffic. Furthermore, we reveal that standalone feature malleability penalization creates a vulnerability trap (surging evasion to 98.9\%), whereas dual hardening combining malleability penalties with Lie-guided adversarial training (MPS + LGAT) slashes FFE evasion to 5.3\% (94.7\% robust recall) with zero clean accuracy degradation.
\end{itemize}
