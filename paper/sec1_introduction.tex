\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) increasingly rely on machine learning over flow-based telemetry. At multi-gigabit rates, inspecting every packet payload is prohibitive, so sensors such as CICFlowMeter \cite{sharafaldin2018toward} aggregate bidirectional packet exchanges into tabular flow records of timing, length, header and flag statistics. Gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs), notably XGBoost \cite{chen2016xgboost}, LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} and CatBoost \cite{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, are the dominant classifiers for these records.

Near-perfect accuracy on benchmark corpora does not guarantee robustness in operation. Closed-world evaluations reward models that exploit testbed artifacts \cite{sommer2010outside} and shortcut features \cite{geirhos2020shortcut}. Attackers, however, control the malleable parts of their traffic, such as timing, padding and window sizes, so a detector that relies on these features can be evaded without losing the exploit \cite{apruzzese2022modeling}.

Evading a tree ensemble is hard for two reasons. First, the ensemble is piecewise constant, so $\nabla_x F(x)=0$ for almost all $x$ and gradient-based attacks receive no signal. Second, flow features obey conservation laws. Flow Bytes/s satisfies $x_8 = 10^6(x_4+x_5)/x_6$, where $x_4$ and $x_5$ are the forward and backward byte totals and $x_6$ is the duration in microseconds. A perturbation that changes the byte totals without updating the rate produces a phantom flow that protocol sanity checks reject \cite{pierazzi2020intrusion, apruzzese2022modeling}.

Existing work follows one of two paradigms, and neither is adequate. Feature-space methods such as ZOO \cite{chen2017zoo} and HopSkipJump \cite{chen2020hopskipjump} ignore the physical coupling: on the complete CSE-CIC-IDS2018, CIC-IDS2017 and UNSW-NB15 captures, HopSkipJump yields no feasible evasive flow at all (100.0\% nominal evasion on two corpora, 0.0\% Real ASR on all three) and ZOO keeps at most 25.2\% Real ASR. Constraint-aware search such as MOEVA, the search stage of CAA \cite{simonetto2024constrained, ghamizi2020moeva}, and packet-level swarm search such as Traffic Manipulator \cite{han2021traffic} respect more of the physics, but query the classifier as a black box, needing 183--445 queries per flow. Recent surrogate-based attacks \cite{simonetto2024constrained, zolbayar2022nidsgan} are cheap at attack time but depend on how closely a surrogate matches the target.

This article proposes Flow-Field Evasion (FFE), which combines both properties. FFE derives physical perturbations as differential generators, so the continuous flow satisfies the invariants exactly and a projection enforces them on the discrete schema, and it steers flows using the split geometry of the ensemble. To our knowledge, no prior attack on tree ensembles combines these two properties; the white-box tree attacks of the literature are cited but were not run as controls (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

The main contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item We model four packet-level attacker actions (temporal dilation, payload padding, chaff injection and TCP window morphing) as closed-form infinitesimal generators of a Lie cone on the flow manifold.
    \item We prove that trajectories in this cone preserve the rate, mean-length, header and MTU invariants of flow records in the continuous relaxation of the flow manifold.
    \item We present FFE, which moves each flow along the generator with the largest cost-weighted score decrease and finishes with tree-split boundary snapping (TSBS).
    \item We evaluate FFE at full scale on the complete, deduplicated CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (5.0 million flows), CIC-IDS2017 (1.7 million) and UNSW-NB15 (124 thousand) captures against XGBoost and LightGBM, with seven baselines. FFE reaches a Real ASR of 99.5\%, 99.2\% and 57.6\% against XGBoost with 100.0\% schema feasibility and 14.6--93.7 queries per flow, significantly above all baselines after Holm correction. It beats surrogate-based CAPGD (88.5\%, 89.5\%, 21.1\%) in 14 of 15 retrained runs and ties in the 15th, but CAPGD needs far fewer target queries and boundary snapping adds at most three points, so most of the success comes from the Lie-cone steering. LightGBM on CIC-IDS2017 is evaded in 35.1\% of the cohort against 99.2\% for XGBoost.
    \item Lie-guided adversarial training halves FFE success at the budget used in training (84.1\% to 46.5\%) but protects much less against a larger budget (98.9\% to 80.3\%) or CAPGD (88.3\% to 74.3\%); combined with hiding the malleable features it is the most robust against those stronger attacks (62.0\% and 55.1\%), and no tested defense brings evasion below 46\%.
\end{itemize}
