\section{Related Work and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:sota}

\subsection{Related Work and State-of-the-Art Taxonomy}
\label{sec:related_work}

Gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs), including XGBoost \cite{chen2016xgboost}, LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} and CatBoost \cite{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, are the standard choice for flow-based network intrusion detection on benchmarks such as CSE-CIC-IDS2018 \cite{sharafaldin2018toward}, UNSW-NB15 \cite{moustafa2015unsw} and CIDDS-001 \cite{ring2017flow}. Comprehensive empirical studies support this choice \cite{dang2022using, dang2023multilayer, apruzzese2022modeling}. Their split rules depend only on the rank order of feature values and are cheap to evaluate, which makes them attractive for deployment. Learned detectors have long been questioned for operation outside the closed world of their training data \cite{sommer2010outside}, but robustness to realistic evasion has received less attention.

Early adversarial attacks on NIDS were transplanted from image classification. Szegedy et al.~\cite{szegedy2014intriguing}, Goodfellow et al.~\cite{goodfellow2015explaining} and Papernot et al.~\cite{papernot2016limitations} showed that gradient-based perturbations fool deep networks, and optimizers such as the Carlini-Wagner $L_2$ attack \cite{carlini2017towards} were then applied to tabular flow features. These studies reported high success rates, but the perturbed vectors were not network flows. Continuous optimization in Euclidean space assigns fractional values to integer packet counts, modifies immutable fields such as destination port, produces negative durations, and breaks the conservation between byte totals and packet counts. A feature vector that no valid packet sequence can produce says nothing about the deployed detector.

The problem-space view corrects this. Pierazzi et al.~\cite{pierazzi2020intrusion} showed that an adversarial perturbation is meaningful only when it is realized as a transformation of the physical object, such as a binary or a packet sequence, and that the transformation must preserve functionality and survive the feature extractor. Verkerken et al.~\cite{verkerken2026problem} apply this view to defining host-space adversarial perturbations against NIDS. Surveys and audits of this literature~\cite{apruzzese2022modeling, apruzzese2023real, he2023adversarial, vitorino2023sok} argue that many feature-space attacks assume threat models that cannot be realized with valid packets under RFC protocol specifications, so their success rates overstate the risk once evaluated against true packet parsers and middleboxes. Constrained tabular attacks address feasibility only in part. CAA \cite{simonetto2024constrained} combines a gradient-based attack (CAPGD) with MOEVA \cite{ghamizi2020moeva}, a multi-objective genetic search whose constraint penalties are heuristic and whose population search receives no directional guidance. TabularBench \cite{simonetto2024tabularbench} standardizes such evaluations across deep and tree models. Packet-level tools avoid these problems by operating on real traffic. Traffic Manipulator \cite{han2021traffic} uses particle swarm optimization to mutate PCAP files by perturbing inter-packet intervals, adding payload bytes and injecting chaff packets. IDSGAN \cite{lin2022idsgan} generates adversarial flow records with generative adversarial networks, but its feasibility is only approximated in feature space.

Exact methods exploit the piecewise-constant structure of trees. Kantchelian et al.~\cite{kantchelian2016evasion} cast minimal evasion against tree ensembles as a mixed-integer linear program. The formulation is exact but NP-hard in general and accepts only linear constraints, so it cannot express the nonlinear rate relations between bytes and duration that flow records obey. SAT and MaxSAT encodings of tree ensembles \cite{ignatiev2022formal, izza2022tackling} provide formal explanations and robustness checks, and LT-Attack \cite{zhang2020efficient} attacks tree ensembles through continuous leaf approximations. The MILP and LT-Attack operate in unconstrained feature space and do not enforce coupled network invariants. Robust training of tree ensembles \cite{chen2019robust, andriushchenko2019provably, calzavara2020treant} and adversarial retraining of neural NIDS \cite{hashemi2020enhancing} improve robustness within the same feature-space threat model. Black-box query methods such as ZOO \cite{chen2017zoo} and HopSkipJump \cite{chen2020hopskipjump} estimate the decision boundary by sampling the classifier's output, and population or swarm searches mutate parameters without knowing which splits are active.

Table~\ref{tab:sota_taxonomy_expanded} summarizes these paradigms and the gap they leave. Methods that exploit the structure of tree ensembles or work in unconstrained feature space do not produce flows that survive packet parsing, while methods that preserve physical feasibility treat the detector as a black box and need hundreds to thousands of queries per flow. Flow-Field Evasion (FFE) addresses this gap by steering flows along infinitesimal packet-level generators that keep every step on the flow manifold, and by using the split geometry of the trees to choose the direction.
