\section{Related Work and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:sota}

\subsection{Related Work and State-of-the-Art Taxonomy}
\label{sec:related_work}

Gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs), including XGBoost \cite{chen2016xgboost}, LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} and CatBoost \cite{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, are the standard choice for flow-based network intrusion detection on benchmarks such as CSE-CIC-IDS2018 \cite{sharafaldin2018toward}, UNSW-NB15 \cite{moustafa2015unsw} and CIDDS-001 \cite{ring2017flow}. Comprehensive empirical studies support this choice \cite{dang2022using, dang2022studying}.

Early adversarial attacks on NIDS were transplanted from image classification. In image classification, Szegedy et al.~\cite{szegedy2014intriguing}, Goodfellow et al.~\cite{goodfellow2015explaining} and Papernot et al.~\cite{papernot2016limitations} showed that gradient-based perturbations fool deep networks, and optimizers such as the Carlini-Wagner $L_2$ attack \cite{carlini2017towards} followed. Applied to flow features, such methods reported high success rates, but the perturbed vectors were not network flows: they assign fractional values to packet counts, modify immutable fields such as destination port, and break the conservation between byte totals and packet counts. The problem-space view corrects this. Pierazzi et al.~\cite{pierazzi2020intrusion} showed that an adversarial perturbation is meaningful only when it is realized as a transformation of the physical object, such as a binary or a packet sequence, and that the transformation must preserve functionality and survive the feature extractor. Verkerken et al.~\cite{verkerken2026problem} apply it to host-space perturbations against NIDS. Surveys and audits of this literature~\cite{apruzzese2023real, he2023adversarial, vitorino2023sok} argue that many attacks assume threat models that attackers cannot realize, and that success rates overstate the risk once realistic constraints are enforced~\cite{apruzzese2022modeling}. Constrained tabular attacks address feasibility only in part: CAA \cite{simonetto2024constrained} combines a gradient-based attack (CAPGD) with the MOEVA genetic search \cite{ghamizi2020moeva}, which handles constraints by penalties, and Traffic Manipulator \cite{han2021traffic} mutates PCAP files with particle swarm search over intervals, padding and chaff.

Recent work makes the realism requirement concrete for NIDS. Merzouk et al.~\cite{merzouk2022practicality} show that many published evasion attacks cannot be realized on live traffic, Sheatsley et al.~\cite{sheatsley2022adversarial} and Debicha et al.~\cite{debicha2023advbot, debicha2026targeted} craft domain-constrained or botnet flows that remain valid, Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025explainable} build explainable and transferable attacks, and El Shehaby et al.~\cite{elshehaby2026perturbability} score which flow features an attacker can perturb, the mutable/immutable split that FFE builds into its schema. Learned generators~\cite{lin2022idsgan, zolbayar2022nidsgan} trade query cost for a training phase. For tabular data in general, CAPGD and CAA~\cite{simonetto2024constrained}, cost-aware attacks~\cite{kireev2023cost} and recent benchmarks and surveys~\cite{simonetto2024tabularbench, he2026tabattackbench, dyrmishi2025insights, vitorino2024benchmark} show that constraint handling dominates reported success rates. The tree-ensemble literature moved toward faster and deployable attacks~\cite{cascioli2024faster}, evasion detection~\cite{devos2023detecting}, and robust training~\cite{vos2021efficient, vos2022robust, calzavara2023verifiable, calzavara2025verifiableboosted, lindqvist2025adversarial}. None of these exploits split geometry while keeping coupled flow invariants exact.

Evaluation practice has also been questioned: CIC-IDS2017 and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 contain labeling errors and duplicated records~\cite{engelen2021troubleshooting, lanvin2023errors, liu2022error}, and detectors generalize poorly across datasets~\cite{cantone2024crossdataset}. We therefore evaluate on complete, deduplicated captures (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

Exact methods exploit the piecewise-constant structure of trees. Kantchelian et al.~\cite{kantchelian2016evasion} cast minimal evasion against tree ensembles as a mixed-integer linear program. The formulation is exact but NP-hard and accepts only linear constraints, so it cannot express the nonlinear rate relations of flow records. MaxSAT encodings of tree ensembles \cite{ignatiev2022formal} and SAT-based explanations of decision trees \cite{izza2022tackling} provide formal explanations and robustness checks, and LT-Attack \cite{zhang2020efficient} attacks tree ensembles through a discrete search over leaf tuples. The MILP and LT-Attack operate in unconstrained feature space and do not enforce coupled network invariants. Robust training of tree ensembles \cite{chen2019robust, andriushchenko2019provably, calzavara2020treant} and adversarial retraining of neural NIDS \cite{hashemi2020enhancing} improve robustness within the same feature-space threat model.

Table~\ref{tab:sota_taxonomy_expanded} summarizes these paradigms and the gap they leave. Methods that exploit the structure of tree ensembles or work in unconstrained feature space do not produce flows that survive packet parsing, while methods that preserve physical feasibility either treat the detector as a black box and need hundreds of queries per flow, or train a surrogate and transfer, which is cheap per flow but depends on how well the surrogate mimics the target. Flow-Field Evasion (FFE) addresses this gap by steering flows along infinitesimal packet-level generators that keep every step on the flow manifold, and by using the split geometry of the trees to choose the direction.
