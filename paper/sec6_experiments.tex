\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}
\label{sec:empirical_evaluation}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Benchmark Corpora}
\label{sec:setup}

Single-testbed evaluations have limited external validity~\cite{apruzzese2022modeling, apruzzese2023real, he2023adversarial, vitorino2023sok, cantone2024crossdataset}, so we use three widely studied corpora in full: CSE-CIC-IDS2018~\cite{sharafaldin2018toward} (the nine daily ``Processed Traffic Data for ML Algorithms'' files, seven of which are capped at 1,048,575 rows in the distributed files), CIC-IDS2017~\cite{sharafaldin2018toward} (five daily CICFlowMeter files) and UNSW-NB15~\cite{moustafa2015unsw} (the official training and testing partition, 257,673 records, a subset of the 2.54 million records of the complete capture). Every record is read and none is subsampled; flows are mapped onto the 12-feature schema of Section~\ref{sec:formulation}.

Cleaning is identical for all corpora. Records with missing or infinite values, non-positive duration, no forward packet, or a protocol other than TCP or UDP are dropped; records that share all 12 features and the label are collapsed to one; records that share the 12 features with conflicting labels are removed, since they have no usable ground truth. The remaining flows are split 70\%/10\%/20\% into training, validation and test partitions, stratified by label and attack family. Deduplicating before splitting keeps any flow from appearing on both sides, a known source of inflated accuracy~\cite{engelen2021troubleshooting, lanvin2023errors, liu2022error}. Duplicates and conflicts remove 33.5\% of the valid CSE-CIC-IDS2018 records, 19.3\% of CIC-IDS2017 and 44.2\% of UNSW-NB15 (Table~\ref{tab:corpora}); the 12-feature mapping of UNSW-NB15 is lossy, so 15,702 of its records are indistinguishable from a record of the opposite class. The invariant projection of Section~\ref{sec:formulation} is applied to every flow and changes a feature value by more than 5\% in 5.8\% of the CIC-IDS2017 and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 records and in none of UNSW-NB15.

The targets are XGBoost (CUDA histogram method, trained on the whole training partition) and LightGBM, each with 60 trees of depth 6 and learning rate 0.1. Attack queries run on the CPU.

We compare FFE with seven methods. \emph{Naive} coordinate descent in $\mathbb{R}^d$ (step $0.05\,\sigma_f$) checks no constraint. \emph{ZOO-NIDS}~\cite{chen2017zoo} and \emph{HopSkipJump-NIDS}~\cite{chen2020hopskipjump} are the zeroth-order and decision-based black-box attacks. \emph{CAA/MOEVA}~\cite{simonetto2024constrained, ghamizi2020moeva} is multi-objective evolutionary search (population 25, 20 generations). \emph{Traffic Manipulator PSO}~\cite{han2021traffic} is particle swarm search over intervals and dummy bytes (15 particles, 25 iterations); its candidates are projected onto the manifold with our closed-form invariants, so it faces the same constraints as FFE. \emph{CAPGD}~\cite{simonetto2024constrained} is our re-implementation of constrained adaptive PGD with momentum and step halving: the attacker trains its own MLP surrogate (two hidden layers of 128 units) on the training flows, takes sign-gradient steps in a log-standardized space ($L_\infty$ budget 1.0, no step on immutable features), repairs every candidate onto the manifold, and queries the target once per iteration (at most 20), stopping at the first evasive candidate. The \emph{NIDSGAN-style generator}~\cite{zolbayar2022nidsgan} is a generator network trained against the same surrogate to emit a bounded perturbation, repaired with the same operator, with at most five noise draws queried against the target; it follows the published idea but is not the authors' code. \emph{FFE} uses step budget $M=20$. We did not run the white-box tree attacks of Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2020efficient} and Cascioli et al.~\cite{cascioli2024faster}, which need the tree structure and do not model flow constraints.

ASR is the fraction of attacks with $P(\text{Malicious} \mid x^*) < 0.5$. PFR is the fraction of candidate flows that pass the protocol, non-negativity and exact coupled-rate checks, that is, $x^* \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{flow}}$. Real ASR counts an attack only when it both evades and is feasible, $\text{Real ASR} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i \mathbb{I}[F(x_i^*) < 0 \land x_i^* \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{flow}}]$. We report Wilson 95\% intervals and exact McNemar tests on paired per-flow outcomes. The cost column is the mean operational cost $\sum_j c_j |x^*_j - x_j|$ with the per-feature unit costs of the schema (payload bytes and delay are cheap, forward packets cost one unit, immutable features 1,000). FFE steers by exactly this quantity and the baselines (except MOEVA) do not optimize it, so the column shows the operational price of the evasion each method finds, not an optimized head-to-head comparison.

Cohorts are drawn once with a fixed seed from the malicious test flows that the target detects. Every method except CAA/MOEVA and PSO attacks the same 1,000 flows; those two need 250--430 queries per flow and attack the first 200 of them, and the McNemar comparisons against them use these 200. The ablation, transferability and defense studies use a balanced cohort of up to 50 detected flows per family of CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (300 flows). All results use one training seed, so the intervals capture sampling variation of the cohort but not retraining variance (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
