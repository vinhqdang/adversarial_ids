\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}
\label{sec:empirical_evaluation}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Benchmark Corpora}
\label{sec:setup}

Single-testbed evaluations have limited external validity~\cite{cantone2024crossdataset}, and attack evaluations that ignore realism overstate the risk~\cite{apruzzese2022modeling, apruzzese2023real, he2023adversarial, vitorino2023sok}, so we use three widely studied corpora in full: CSE-CIC-IDS2018~\cite{sharafaldin2018toward} (nine daily files, seven of them capped at 1,048,575 rows in the distribution), CIC-IDS2017~\cite{sharafaldin2018toward} (five daily files) and UNSW-NB15~\cite{moustafa2015unsw} (the official training and testing partition, 257,673 of the 2.54 million records of the complete capture). Every record is read and mapped onto the 12-feature schema of Section~\ref{sec:formulation}.

Cleaning is identical for all corpora. We drop records with missing or infinite values, non-positive duration, no forward packet or a protocol other than TCP or UDP, and TCP flows without an observed window; the window of UDP flows, recorded as $-1$ by CICFlowMeter, is set to 0. In CIC-IDS2017 we remove the 11,974 ``Attempted'' probes and count ``Infiltration -- Portscan'' as port scanning; in UNSW-NB15 we drop zero-duration flows. Every flow is projected onto the flow manifold (Section~\ref{sec:formulation}), which changes a feature by more than 5\% in 3.1\% of the valid CSE-CIC-IDS2018 records, 4.7\% of CIC-IDS2017 and none of UNSW-NB15. Records with identical features and label are then collapsed so that no flow appears on both sides of the split, a known source of inflated accuracy~\cite{engelen2021troubleshooting, lanvin2023errors, liu2022error}: this removes 39.1\%, 20.5\% and 42.7\% of the valid records and leaves 4,968,389, 1,658,869 and 123,508 flows (Supplementary Table~S5). Records with identical features but different labels (149,526, 22 and 1,040) are kept as real ambiguity of the schema. The split into training, validation (unused) and test partitions is 70\%/10\%/20\%, stratified by label and family, random, and not grouped by time or host, so near-duplicates may fall on both sides and accuracies are optimistic.

The targets are XGBoost (CUDA, trained on the whole training partition) and LightGBM, each with 60 trees of depth 6 and learning rate 0.1. The methods differ in what the attacker knows. The full FFE reads the split thresholds for its snapping step (Tier~1), and we count every probe and snapping candidate as a query although a white-box attacker could evaluate its local model for free; FFE without snapping uses only the soft score, as do CAPGD and ZOO, whereas HopSkipJump uses only decisions. \emph{ZOO-NIDS}~\cite{chen2017zoo}, \emph{HopSkipJump-NIDS}~\cite{chen2020hopskipjump} (30 iterations, 47 queries), \emph{MOEVA}~\cite{simonetto2024constrained, ghamizi2020moeva} (the search stage of CAA; population 25, 20 generations) and \emph{PSO}~\cite{han2021traffic} (feature-space swarm over interval and padding knobs, 15 particles, 25 iterations) only query the target. \emph{CAPGD}~\cite{simonetto2024constrained} and the \emph{NIDSGAN-style generator}~\cite{zolbayar2022nidsgan} are our re-implementations: the attacker trains its own MLP (two hidden layers of 128 units) on the training flows, takes gradients on it, and queries the target once per iteration or draw (at most 20 and 5); surrogate computations are not counted. The \emph{naive} attack is coordinate descent in $\mathbb{R}^d$. The naive attack, ZOO and HopSkipJump are unconstrained by design, MOEVA penalizes violations without repairing them, and PSO (with the closed-form projection that we added), CAPGD and the generator repair every candidate onto the manifold. Baselines use the default budgets above. The white-box tree attacks of Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2020efficient} and Cascioli et al.~\cite{cascioli2024faster} need the tree structure and do not model flow constraints; the official LT-Attack code needs a Bazel and C++ build that we did not set up, so as a control we wrote a greedy leaf-tuple-style attack on the XGBoost trees, run with a projection after the attack and with a projection of every candidate (Supplementary Section~S7). It is not LT-Attack, and we did not run Cascioli et al.

ASR is the fraction of attacks with $P(\text{Malicious} \mid x^*) < 0.5$; PFR is the fraction of the evasive candidates that satisfy the schema invariants $x^* \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{flow}}$; Real ASR counts an attack only when it both evades and is feasible. Feasibility is checked against the invariants that FFE's projection enforces, so for FFE it holds by construction and means conformance to the schema; a synthetic packet replay is reported in Supplementary Section~S8. We report Wilson 95\% intervals and exact McNemar tests on paired outcomes with Holm correction over the 21 comparisons. Cost is the median over a method's own feasible evasions of the operational cost of Section~\ref{sec:cost} and of the feature-normalized distance $D_{L_1} = \frac{1}{d}\sum_j |x^*_j - x_j|/\sigma_j$, both computed identically for every method; the medians cover different flows and are not a paired comparison.

Cohorts are drawn once, with a fixed seed, from the malicious test flows that both XGBoost and LightGBM detect when clean (77.6\%, 99.8\% and 87.0\% of the test attacks of CSE-CIC-IDS2018, CIC-IDS2017 and UNSW-NB15), so all results are conditional on detection. Every method except MOEVA and PSO attacks the same 1,000 flows; those two need 183--445 queries per flow and attack the first 200 of them, and the paired tests against them use these 200. The main results use one training seed; Section~\ref{sec:ablation} repeats the comparison for five seeds. The ablation, transferability and defense studies use a balanced cohort of up to 50 detected flows per family of CSE-CIC-IDS2018.
