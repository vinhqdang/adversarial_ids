Table~\ref{tab:master_evasion_benchmark} compares FFE with the five baselines on real network flows. Several baselines evade the detector mathematically but produce physically unrealizable flow telemetry. For example, HopSkipJump-NIDS achieves 100.0\% unconstrained ASR, yet achieves 0.0\% Physical Feasibility Rate (PFR) and 0.0\% Real ASR because it projects candidate flows into non-physical coordinates with an astronomical perturbation cost ($\bar{D}_{L_1} = 1.64\times 10^6$). Similarly, ZOO-NIDS and CAA/MOEVA achieve only 0.0\% and 1.0\% Real ASR (with PFRs of 6.0\% and 15.0\%), demonstrating that unconstrained mutations severely decouple conservation laws and transport header invariants.

In contrast, Traffic Manipulator PSO and Flow-Field Evasion operate strictly within valid physical flow semantics, achieving 100.0\% PFR. However, FFE outperforms Traffic Manipulator PSO in Real ASR (59.0\% vs. 54.0\%) while requiring less than half the query complexity (79.3 vs. 182.6 queries per flow) and over 7,500$\times$ lower normalized perturbation cost ($\bar{D}_{L_1} = 0.392$ vs. 2,946.18). FFE achieves this efficiency by navigating along infinitesimal Lie generators and snapping to tree-split boundaries via TSBS, rather than relying on stochastic swarm dispersion.

As shown in Panel A, FFE exhibits distinct physical evasion behavior depending on the flow geometry of each attack family. Low-and-slow infiltration (100.0\% Real ASR, 6.5 queries) and web application exploits (100.0\% Real ASR, 12.4 queries) evade with high efficiency via window morphing and chaff injection. UDP volumetric floods achieve 96.0\% Real ASR at 30.3 queries via rate dilution ($\vec{v}_{\text{time}}$). Conversely, DoS SYN floods and SSH/FTP brute force present strict physical hurdles on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 due to deep tree splits on destination port (which is immutable) and fixed packet length bounds, requiring additional budget.

Panel B demonstrates the cross-dataset validity of FFE across three independent benchmark datasets and two leading gradient boosting implementations (XGBoost and LightGBM). On UNSW-NB15, FFE achieves 98.0\% and 94.0\% Real ASR on XGBoost and LightGBM, respectively, with moderate query budgets (32.2 and 34.3 queries). On CSE-CIC-IDS2018, LightGBM is substantially more vulnerable than XGBoost (97.0\% vs. 59.0\% Real ASR), requiring only 10.4 queries per flow and 15.5 ms latency. On CIC-IDS2017, where the tree ensembles achieve near-perfect discrimination (99.87\%--99.90\% clean accuracy and 100.00\% AUC), Real ASR drops to 11.0\% (XGBoost) and 3.0\% (LightGBM), illustrating how tree split depth and feature distributions impact evasion feasibility. Across all datasets and models, CUDA XGBoost incurs higher per-sample latency than LightGBM due to host-to-device memory transfers on single-row inference.
