Table~\ref{tab:master_evasion_benchmark} compares FFE with seven baselines on the three full-scale corpora. Evading the model in feature space is not the same as producing a feasible flow, but the gap differs by method. HopSkipJump-NIDS reaches 100.0\% ASR on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CIC-IDS2017 yet none of its evasive candidates is feasible. The naive attack and ZOO-NIDS keep only 34.4\% and 35.0\% (CIC-IDS2017) and 0.9\% and 0.0\% (UNSW-NB15) of their evasions as feasible, but 88.0\% and 83.2\% on CSE-CIC-IDS2018, at a median operational cost of essentially zero; this is consistent with moving only the coupled rate features within the 5\% tolerance of the consistency check, which would mean that the check is not exact and flatters these baselines. MOEVA, which penalizes but does not repair violations, ends with a feasible share of 59.4\%--100.0\%.

FFE has the highest Real ASR on all three corpora: 99.5\%, 99.2\% and 57.6\%. All 21 comparisons with the baselines are significant after Holm correction (largest adjusted exact McNemar $p = 3.7\times10^{-11}$). The margin over the best baseline is 11.0 points on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (CAPGD, 88.5\%; the 110 flows that only FFE evades against none that only CAPGD evades), 9.7 points on CIC-IDS2017 (CAPGD, 89.5\%; 97 against none) and, on UNSW-NB15, 22 points over PSO on the 200 flows both attacked (64.0\% against 42.0\%; 47 against 3), where CAPGD reaches only 21.1\%. CAPGD's surrogate agrees with the target on 99.8\% and 99.9\% of the test flows of CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CIC-IDS2017 but on only 87.6\% of UNSW-NB15 (Table~\ref{tab:multidataset_benchmark}).

The methods trade target queries, knowledge and cost differently. CAPGD and the NIDSGAN-style generator need far fewer target queries than FFE (3.2--16.2 and 2.4--4.5 against 14.6--93.7), since their gradient work runs on a pre-trained surrogate that is not counted. FFE needs no surrogate; its snapping step reads the split thresholds, but the score-only variant of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} does not. On the same 200 flows FFE needs 2.8--20 times fewer queries than PSO and MOEVA, although query counts are not strictly like-for-like across methods that use scores, decisions or a surrogate. The median operational cost of FFE's feasible evasions is 0.001, 0.001 and 5.6 units, against 15.2--21.6 for PSO, 15.0--53.2 for CAPGD and 24.6--48.0 for the generator. This is partly by design, since FFE steers by this cost, and it is specific to this cost model: in the feature-normalized distance FFE (0.116, 0.289, 0.027) is not smaller than PSO (0.061, 0.137, 0.060) on two of the three corpora.

Table~\ref{tab:multidataset_benchmark} shows that clean accuracy is 97.30\% on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (F1 87.3\%, since 12.0\% of the flows are attacks and the rare families are hard), 99.96\% on CIC-IDS2017 and 88.51\% on UNSW-NB15, where the 12-feature mapping is lossy and 13.0\% of the test attacks are missed by at least one of the two models. On CSE-CIC-IDS2018 the XGBoost model places the root split of 51 of its 60 trees on the destination port, an immutable feature, and is nevertheless evaded in 99.5\% of the attacked flows, so immutability of the root feature does not by itself protect a model. On CIC-IDS2017 FFE succeeds far less often against LightGBM than against XGBoost (35.1\% against 99.2\%, with 120.1 against 14.6 queries). Of the XGBoost trees there, 36 of 60 split first on the initial window, a feature that FFE can move, 14 on the protocol and 10 on backward payload bytes; we did not analyze the LightGBM trees and cannot say whether the lower figure reflects a more robust model or an attack that fits its splits poorly. Per-family results are in Supplementary Table~S1: on UNSW-NB15 exploits, more than half of the cohort, are evaded in only 44.6\% of 504 flows and set the aggregate figure.
