Table~\ref{tab:master_evasion_benchmark} compares FFE with the five baselines on real network flows. Several baselines evade the detector mathematically but produce physically unrealizable flow telemetry. For example, HopSkipJump-NIDS achieves 100.0\% unconstrained ASR, yet achieves 0.0\% Physical Feasibility Rate (PFR) and 0.0\% Real ASR because it projects candidate flows into non-physical coordinates with an astronomical perturbation cost ($\bar{D}_{L_1} = 1.64\times 10^6$). Similarly, ZOO-NIDS and CAA/MOEVA achieve only 0.0\% and 1.0\% Real ASR (with PFRs of 6.0\% and 15.0\%), demonstrating that unconstrained mutations severely decouple conservation laws and transport header invariants.

Both Traffic Manipulator PSO and Flow-Field Evasion operate strictly within valid physical flow semantics, achieving 100.0\% PFR and reaching comparable nominal evasion rates on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (59.0\% for FFE vs. 54.0\% for PSO). In our benchmark, PSO enforces 100.0\% PFR via the same invariant projection step. Given the evaluation cohort of $N=100$, the 5.0\% difference in ASR lies within overlapping binomial 95\% confidence intervals (49.2\%--68.2\% vs. 44.3\%--63.4\%, $p = 0.48$). However, their operational footprints diverge by orders of magnitude: PSO requires $2.3\times$ higher query complexity (182.6 vs. 79.3 queries) and incurs an astronomical $7,500\times$ higher perturbation overhead ($\bar{D}_{L_1} = 2,946.18$ vs. $0.392$) via unguided stochastic search. In contrast, FFE navigates along infinitesimal Lie generators and snaps to the nearest boundary partition via TSBS. FFE's primary advantage over packet-space heuristics therefore lies in surgical geometric boundary traversal rather than brute-force evasion margins.

As shown in Panel A, FFE exhibits distinct physical evasion behavior depending on the flow geometry of each attack family. Low-and-slow infiltration (100.0\% Real ASR, 6.5 queries) and web application exploits (100.0\% Real ASR, 12.4 queries) evade with high efficiency via window morphing and chaff injection. UDP volumetric floods achieve 96.0\% Real ASR at 30.3 queries via rate dilution ($\vec{v}_{\text{time}}$). Conversely, DoS SYN floods and SSH/FTP brute force present strict physical hurdles on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 due to deep tree splits on destination port (which is immutable) and fixed packet length bounds, requiring additional budget.

Panel B validates FFE across three benchmark datasets on XGBoost and LightGBM. On UNSW-NB15, FFE achieves 98.0\% and 94.0\% Real ASR on XGBoost and LightGBM (32.2 and 34.3 queries). On CSE-CIC-IDS2018, LightGBM is notably more vulnerable than XGBoost (97.0\% vs. 59.0\% Real ASR, 10.4 queries). On CIC-IDS2017, where ensembles achieve near-perfect clean accuracy (99.87\%--99.90\%), Real ASR drops to 11.0\% (XGBoost) and 3.0\% (LightGBM) with 93.0\% PFR. Empirical tree inspection reveals the structural barrier: 52 of 60 XGBoost trees root-split on immutable backward payload bytes ($\text{totlen\_bwd\_pkts}$, mean gain 781.1), with depth-1 splits dominated by backward packets ($\text{tot\_bwd\_pkts}$) and destination port. Because backward traffic and destination ports are immutable under ingress inspection, forward Lie steering cannot bridge these root partitions, restricting Real ASR to 3.0\%--11.0\%. Higher XGBoost latency reflects host-to-device transfers on single-row inference.
