\paragraph{Metric Definitions}
In Table~\ref{tab:master_evasion_benchmark}, we report six performance dimensions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Attack Success Rate (ASR):} The percentage of attacks where the model output probability drops below the detection threshold ($P(\text{Malicious} \mid x^*) < 0.5$).
    \item \textbf{Physical Feasibility Rate (PFR):} The percentage of candidate flows that pass all protocol sanity checks, non-negative packet counts, and exact coupled rate equations ($x^* \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{flow}}$). For unconstrained baseline attacks that fail to evade or get trapped in infeasible regions, PFR is evaluated across evasive candidates.
    \item \textbf{Real Attack Success Rate (Real ASR):} The percentage of attacks that simultaneously achieve both classifier evasion \emph{and} physical feasibility:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Real ASR} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}\left[ F(x_i^*) < 0 \;\land\; x_i^* \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{flow}} \right]
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Average Queries:} Mean number of model evaluations required per sample.
    \item \textbf{Average Perturbation Cost ($\bar{D}_{L_1}$):} Dimensionless normalized perturbation distance across all $d=12$ features:
    \begin{equation}
        \bar{D}_{L_1}(x^*, x) = \frac{1}{d} \sum_{j=1}^d \frac{|x_j^* - x_j|}{\sigma_j}
    \end{equation}
    where $\sigma_j$ is the empirical standard deviation of feature $j$ across the training corpus. FFE achieves minimal perturbation ($\bar{D}_{L_1} = 0.048$, representing a mean shift of only $0.048$ feature standard deviations across the $d=12$ telemetry features), whereas unconstrained and heuristic search baselines induce large normalized distortions ($\bar{D}_{L_1} \in [0.248, 0.512]$) to cross decision boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Total Execution Time:} Wall-clock time required to evaluate all 100 attack flows on the GPU workstation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Detailed Empirical Findings}

\paragraph{The Complete Collapse of Real ASR in Existing Methods}
While unconstrained algorithms claim high mathematical evasion in theoretical papers, their \textbf{Real ASR} collapses in practice. The naive coordinate descent method achieves 0.0\% Real ASR because it gets trapped in local minima. ZOO-NIDS and HopSkipJump achieve Real ASRs of only 3.0\% and 8.0\%, respectively; their unconstrained continuous mutations produce decoupled byte/packet rates that fail physical invariant checks.

\paragraph{High-Assurance Evasion with Guaranteed Feasibility (100\% Real ASR)}
Flow-Field Evasion achieves a \textbf{100.0\% Attack Success Rate} and a \textbf{100.0\% Physical Feasibility Rate}, resulting in a verified \textbf{100.0\% Real ASR} (with a 95\% Wilson score confidence interval of $[96.3\%, 100.0\%]$ for $N=100$). Because every infinitesimal step in FFE is constrained to the positive Lie Cone $\mathcal{K}_{\text{net}}(x)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant_universal} guarantees that any synthesized flow trajectory remains strictly inside the physical manifold $\mathcal{M}_{\text{flow}}$ by construction.

\paragraph{Two-Orders-of-Magnitude Query Reduction}
FFE requires an average of only \textbf{12.9 model queries} per sample. In stark contrast:
\begin{itemize}
    \item HopSkipJump requires 680 queries (53$\times$ more queries than FFE).
    \item CAA / MOEVA requires 1,240 queries (96$\times$ more queries than FFE).
    \item Traffic Manipulator PSO requires 1,850 queries (143$\times$ more queries than FFE).
\end{itemize}
This dramatic reduction occurs because FFE evaluates directional Lie derivatives along the four physically admissible generators rather than searching blindly across the 12-dimensional continuous space.

\paragraph{Sub-Second Execution Latency}
FFE executes 100 full attack flows in \textbf{4.37 seconds}, equating to an average algorithmic optimization latency of \textbf{43.7 milliseconds per flow} on the GPU workstation. This wall-clock figure is dominated by single-row XGBoost inference on a CUDA device, which pays a host-to-device transfer per query, and it is not stable across runs: repeated runs of the identical benchmark on the same machine produced totals between 1.5\,s and 4.4\,s, so only order-of-magnitude conclusions should be drawn from it. We distinguish this algorithmic computation latency (43.7 ms) from the physical network transmission delay introduced into the packet stream. Conversely, Traffic Manipulator takes 420.15 seconds (over 7 minutes) in algorithmic runtime alone. This roughly 96$\times$ computational speedup (420.15\,s vs.\ 4.37\,s) makes real-time inline packet manipulation computationally viable on production network interface cards. \subsubsection{Behavioral Analysis by Attack Family}
Averaged over the five equally sized family cohorts ($N=500$ flows), the mean query complexity is $11.6$ queries and the mean latency is $59.7$ ms per flow, comparable to the $12.9$ queries observed on the $N=100$ master-benchmark cohort (which is drawn from the mixed test partition and therefore has a different family composition); the latency of the per-family runs is dominated by single-row CUDA inference overhead. Behavioral breakdown across individual attack profiles reveals distinct optimal Lie generator selections:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Volumetric DoS \& UDP Floods (Families 1 \& 2):} The primary signature exploited by tree splits is extreme flow byte rate ($x_8$) and packet rate ($x_9$). FFE identifies the Temporal Dilation generator $\vec{v}_{\text{time}}$ as having the highest negative Lie derivative per unit cost. Extending flow duration smoothly dilutes byte and packet rates, crossing decision thresholds reliably.
    \item \textbf{Brute Force Authentication (Family 3):} Repetitive credential submissions exhibit fixed forward packet lengths ($x_{10}$). FFE primarily engages the Payload Padding generator $\vec{v}_{\text{pad}}$, injecting dummy application bytes that shift the mean packet length across the model's split boundary in only $5.2$ queries.
    \item \textbf{Web Application Exploits (Family 4):} SQLi and XSS flows exhibit high request-to-response packet asymmetry. FFE injects dummy forward keep-alives via $\vec{v}_{\text{chaff}}$, balancing packet ratios and matching benign HTTP browsing distributions.
    \item \textbf{Infiltration (Family 5):} Covert beacons already have low volumetric footprints but are flagged by distinct botnet window sizes ($x_7$). FFE immediately selects the Window Morphing generator $\vec{v}_{\text{win}}$, flipping the window to standard client OS values ($64,240$) and achieving evasion in only $5.0$ queries.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cross-Dataset Empirical Insights.}
The multi-dataset benchmark in Table~\ref{tab:multidataset_benchmark} highlights four crucial findings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Guaranteed Physical Realizability Across All Testbeds:} The Physical Feasibility Rate (PFR) remains strictly $100.0\%$ on all three benchmark datasets. Because FFE integrates perturbations along the Lie Cone generators $\mathcal{K}_{\text{net}}$, the invariant network conservation laws are strictly preserved across the diverse telemetry distributions modeled by each testbed.
    \item \textbf{Universal Evasion Efficacy ($100.0\%$ Real ASR):} FFE achieves $100.0\%$ Real ASR on CSE-CIC-IDS2018, UNSW-NB15, and CIDDS-001 against both XGBoost and LightGBM. UNSW-NB15 is the costliest testbed ($14.3$ queries for XGBoost, $17.8$ for LightGBM), consistent with its subtler volumetric signatures (e.g., Analysis and Fuzzers), while CIDDS-001 is the cheapest ($6.8$ and $9.3$ queries). The flows evaluated here are synthesized from dataset-calibrated generators (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), not replayed from the original packet captures.
    \item \textbf{Bounded Latency:} Average query counts remain between $6.8$ and $17.8$ queries across all datasets, translating to per-flow latencies between $8.4$ ms and $66.0$ ms (the upper end occurs for CUDA-hosted XGBoost, where every single-row query pays a host-to-device transfer; wall-clock values vary between runs). This confirms that the computational efficiency of directional Lie steering remains consistent across diverse network traffic profiles and varying background noise characteristics.
    \item \textbf{Alignment with Multi-Dataset NIDS Literature:} Prior investigations by Dang~\cite{dang2022detecting, dang2023learning, le2026stfedxids} demonstrated that machine learning classifiers often exhibit poor transferability across disparate network testbeds because decision boundaries overfit to dataset-specific feature distributions. Our findings demonstrate that physical Lie vector field evasion is universally effective across models trained on diverse traffic distributions (enterprise, hybrid, and cloud OpenStack environments), consistently bypassing detection regardless of testbed-specific baseline profiles because tree ensembles across all three environments fundamentally rely on malleable rate and duration metrics to partition intrusion traffic.
\end{enumerate}
