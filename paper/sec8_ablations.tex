\subsection{Retraining variance, ablations and transferability}
\label{sec:ablation}

\paragraph{Retraining variance.} The single-seed benchmark cannot show how much of the ranking is luck of the split and the surrogate. We therefore repeated the whole pipeline for five seeds (new split, new XGBoost model, new surrogate and generator, new cohort of 300 detected flows) for FFE, CAPGD and the NIDSGAN-style generator (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). FFE is at least as successful as CAPGD in all 15 runs and strictly better in 14 (the exception is a tie at 99.3\% on one CSE-CIC-IDS2018 seed). Its spread is also smaller: on CIC-IDS2017 the standard deviation over seeds is 1.5 points for FFE against 11.9 for CAPGD and 15.7 for the generator, whose success depends on the surrogate fitted in that run.

\paragraph{Role of tree-split boundary snapping.} Switching TSBS off (Supplementary Table~S2) leaves Real ASR unchanged on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (99.5\% in both cases) and lowers it from 99.2\% to 97.5\% on CIC-IDS2017 and from 57.6\% to 54.6\% on UNSW-NB15, while the mean number of queries rises from 11.6, 13.5 and 51.5 to 16.5, 14.6 and 93.7 with TSBS. Snapping therefore rescues at most three more points of Real ASR and costs up to 1.8 times as many queries. Most of FFE's success comes from steering along the Lie generators, and we do not claim that boundary snapping makes the attack query-efficient.

\paragraph{Other ablations.} The remaining ablations (Supplementary Section~S4; CSE-CIC-IDS2018, XGBoost, 283 balanced flows) show success rising with the step budget from 42.8\% at $M=1$ (5.0 queries) to 84.1\% at $M=5$ and 98.9\% at $M\ge 10$; no single generator suffices (10.2\%--66.1\%) and only the complete cone reaches 98.9\%; the cost regime and the snapping offset leave it unchanged, so on this saturated corpus they are uninformative.

\paragraph{Transferability.} We crafted FFE flows against one architecture and scored them with the other three, using the 221 malicious flows that all four detect when clean, so that a transfer is not the target's own false negative; all four models share the training partition. Flows crafted against XGBoost evade LightGBM in 71.5\% of the cases and Random Forest in 55.7\%; flows crafted against the other models evade the remaining non-CatBoost targets in 55.7\%--78.7\%. CatBoost is the hardest target: 24.0\% for XGBoost, LightGBM and Random Forest sources alike, although attacks against CatBoost itself succeed in only 69.7\% of the cases, so FFE is also less effective against it directly (Supplementary Table~S4).
