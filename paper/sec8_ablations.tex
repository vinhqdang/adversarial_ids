In Panel A, ASR rises monotonically with step budget, from 14.7\% at $M=1$ (5.0 queries, $\bar{D}_{L_1}=0.165$) to 51.6\% at $M=5$ (28.1 queries), reaching a saturation plateau of 56.8\% at $M=10$ (49.8 queries, $\bar{D}_{L_1}=0.375$). Increasing the budget beyond $M=10$ yields no further evasion on this cohort because the remaining unevaded flows are bounded by immutable port and protocol constraints. Physical feasibility remains 98.9\% across all budgets.

Panel B demonstrates the necessity of the complete Lie cone. Individual generators achieve limited evasion: temporal dilation alone reaches 25.3\% (adding 77.3\,s of flow duration), chaff injection reaches 25.3\%, window morphing reaches 16.8\%, and payload padding reaches 12.6\%. Combining generators produces synergistic gains: Time + Chaff achieves 45.3\%, while the Complete Lie Cone achieves 56.8\% ASR at 84.4 queries. Notably, Padding + Chaff achieves 36.8\% ASR with zero added flow delay (0.00\,s) at only 10.4 queries per flow.

Panel C examines the effect of control cost weighting $\vec{c}$. Across all four operational regimes, FFE consistently achieves 56.8\% ASR with comparable query counts (83.9--84.4 queries), with $\vec{v}_{\text{time}}$ serving as the dominant generator in 85\%--86\% of gradient steps. Sweeping the TSBS relative snapping offset $\epsilon_{\text{rel}}$ from 0 to 1.0 similarly maintains 56.8\% ASR and 98.9\% PFR, confirming stability across threshold snapping tolerances.

In many operational settings the attacker cannot query the deployed model directly. Table~\ref{tab:transferability} therefore evaluates black-box cross-architecture transferability by measuring the attack success rate of flows crafted on a source tree ensemble and evaluated against three independent target architectures ($N=95$ detected flows).

The empirical transferability matrix reveals strong structural cross-vulnerability among tree ensembles on real network traffic. Flows crafted against XGBoost (CUDA) transfer with exceptional effectiveness to LightGBM (95.8\% ASR) and Random Forest (77.9\% ASR), matching CatBoost at 56.8\%. Conversely, LightGBM is universally the most susceptible target architecture to black-box transfer, suffering 80.0\%--95.8\% evasion when attacked by adversarial flows crafted on other ensembles. Flows crafted on CatBoost transfer robustly to both LightGBM (87.4\%) and Random Forest (76.8\%). Transfers into XGBoost range from 25.3\% to 42.1\%, demonstrating that XGBoost's split thresholds on discrete packet length bounds establish narrower transfer corridors than histogram-based leaf-wise trees.
