\subsection{Ablations and transferability}
\label{sec:ablation}

The full ablation and transferability tables are in the Supplementary Material (Sections S4 and S5). We summarize them for the CUDA XGBoost target of CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and a balanced cohort of $N=300$ detected malicious flows.

ASR rises with the step budget from 52.7\% at $M=1$ (5.0 queries) through 96.3\% at $M=5$ to 100.0\% at $M=20$ (10.3 queries), with 100.0\% physical feasibility throughout. No single generator suffices: alone they evade 30.0\% (padding), 47.7\% (chaff), 53.0\% (window morphing) and 57.0\% (temporal dilation, at 33.5 queries and 39.8\,s of added duration), pairs reach 49.0\%--82.7\%, and only the complete cone reaches 100.0\%. The cost regime and the TSBS snapping offset leave ASR at 100.0\%, so on this saturated corpus they are uninformative about success.

In many operational settings the attacker cannot query the deployed model. Flows crafted against XGBoost evade LightGBM in 69.0\% of the cases, Random Forest in 81.3\% and CatBoost in 37.7\%; across the other source models, 54\%--83\% of transferred flows evade every target except CatBoost, which is the hardest target for every source (34.0\%--37.7\%) even though attacks against CatBoost itself succeed in 99.3\% of the cases.
