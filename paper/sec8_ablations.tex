\subsection{Ablations and transferability}
\label{sec:ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation_budget_expanded} reports three ablations on the CUDA XGBoost target of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, using the balanced cohort of $N=100$ detected malicious flows, where one flow corresponds to one percentage point. Panel A varies the step budget $M$, Panel B disables generators, and Panel C varies the cost regime.

ASR rises from 41.0\% at $M=1$ to 95.0\% at $M=5$ and reaches 100.0\% at $M=10$, with 11.6 queries and a normalized cost of $\bar{D}_{L_1}=0.218$. Budgets above 10 change neither ASR, queries nor cost, so $M=20$ and $M=50$ are omitted from the table. The mean cost grows from 0.137 at $M=1$ to 0.218 and then stays flat.

A disabled generator is removed from the directional probes of Supplementary Algorithm~S1 and from the TSBS candidate set of Algorithm~\ref{alg:tsbs}. Temporal dilation alone is the strongest single generator at 67.0\%, but it adds 32.5\,s of flow duration on average. The best pair, time with chaff, reaches 82.0\% at 16.19\,s, and no proper subset reaches 100.0\%. The full cone reaches 100.0\% at 11.6 queries and 0.40\,s of added delay. PFR remains 100.0\% for every subset because the projection enforces the schema relations whichever generators are active.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:ffe_master} selects generators by the score $(-\nabla_k)/c_k$, so the cost vector $\vec{c}$ sets the trade-off. Regime A uses the default cost vector of Section~\ref{sec:cost}. Penalizing delay in Regime B cuts the mean added delay from 396\,ms to 133\,ms at a similar query cost (11.3 vs.\ 11.6), while the normalized cost rises from 0.218 to 0.246. Regime C is nearly identical to Regime A, with 396\,ms in both. All four regimes reach 100.0\% ASR, and $\vec{v}_{\text{time}}$ is the dominant generator in each, where dominance counts only the directional step of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ffe_master} and TSBS edits are not attributed to a generator. Varying the relative snapping offset $\epsilon_{\text{rel}}$ from 0 to 1.0 leaves ASR and PFR at 100.0\% and lowers mean queries only from 11.6 to 11.4.

In many operational settings the attacker cannot query the deployed model directly. Table~\ref{tab:transferability} therefore reports the attack success rate of flows crafted on a source model and evaluated on each target. Off-diagonal entries range from 69\% to 94\%, so flows crafted on one tree ensemble usually also evade the others. We attribute this to the shared reliance of tree ensembles on the same volumetric features that the generators modify, but we did not test this mechanism directly.
