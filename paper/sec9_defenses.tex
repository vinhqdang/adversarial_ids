\section{Defensive Countermeasures}
\label{sec:defense}

Standard boosting picks split features by loss reduction alone, so features that separate the classes in benchmark corpora, such as duration and the rates, can sit near the root although an attacker can shift them by delaying or padding packets. We evaluate two defenses: malleability-penalized splitting (MPS), which keeps the model from relying on malleable features, and Lie-guided adversarial training (LGAT), which trains on flows generated along the Lie generators.

\subsection{Malleability-penalized tree splitting}

Following robust split formulations~\cite{chen2019robust, calzavara2020treant}, the split score of feature $j$ is discounted by its physical malleability index
\begin{equation}
    M_{\text{mal}}(j) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}} \left[ \max_{k \in \{1, \dots, 4\}} \frac{\delta s_k |\vec{v}_k(x)_j|}{\sigma_j} \right],
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_j$ is the feature standard deviation and $\delta s_k$ the perturbation scale of generator $k$ ($\delta s_1 = 10^4\,\mu\text{s}$, $\delta s_2 = 100\,\text{B}$, $\delta s_3 = 1\,\text{pkt}$, $\delta s_4 = 10^3\,\text{B}$). Our experiments evaluate the limit in which the penalty is infinite: the ensemble is trained and queried without the features that temporal dilation changes, namely the duration $x_6$ and the two rates $x_8, x_9$ (the two rates also have by far the largest malleability index). The attacker still perturbs the complete flow and, following the white-box tier, knows the thresholds of the hardened model.

\subsection{Lie-guided adversarial training}

Standard adversarial training assumes an $L_p$ ball around each input~\cite{madry2018towards}, which for network flows does not respect protocol constraints and can generate telemetry that no packet sequence produces~\cite{apruzzese2022modeling}. LGAT instead restricts the inner maximization to the physical flow manifold: FFE flows with budget $M=5$ are generated against the undefended model for a random sample of 5,000 malicious training flows (1.2\% of the 416,000 in the training partition, capped to bound the generation cost) and appended to the training set with the malicious label (Supplementary Algorithm~S4).

\subsection{Evaluation}

We compare the undefended XGBoost model (60 trees of depth 6) with MPS, LGAT and MPS+LGAT on the full CSE-CIC-IDS2018 capture. Each model is attacked with FFE at the training budget $M=5$, with FFE at $M=20$, and with the surrogate-transfer CAPGD baseline, on the balanced cohort of malicious test flows that the undefended model detects (283 flows, up to 50 per family) restricted to the flows that the evaluated model itself still detects (274, 269 and 274 flows for the hardened models). None of the defenses changes clean detection measurably: accuracy is 97.28\%--97.30\%, F1 87.3\% and attack recall 77.6\%--77.8\% for all four models. Against the attack that generated the augmentation, LGAT lowers FFE success from 84.1\% to 46.5\% at $M=5$. That protection does not extend far: at $M=20$ the success of FFE against LGAT is 80.3\% (undefended 98.9\%), and CAPGD reaches 74.3\% (undefended 88.3\%). MPS alone gives a smaller reduction (78.8\% for both FFE budgets, 71.9\% for CAPGD). The combination is the most robust model against the stronger attacks (62.0\% at $M=20$, 55.1\% for CAPGD) but is weaker than LGAT at the training budget (62.0\% against 46.5\%). Differences of a few points between the hardened models, such as 62.0\% against 55.1\%, are within sampling error for 270 flows and one seed. For the two masked models the $M=5$ and $M=20$ rates coincide, so a larger budget does not help FFE once duration and rates are hidden.

No configuration brings evasion below 46\%, so none solves the problem. Our reading is that adversarial training with a small budget buys robustness mainly against the attack it was trained on, and that hiding the malleable features, which costs nothing in clean performance here, helps less alone but makes the model harder to evade with a larger budget or a surrogate attack when combined with adversarial training. These are hypotheses: we ran one seed, one corpus and two attack families, and the attacker is white-box with respect to the hardened model but not adaptive to its training procedure.
