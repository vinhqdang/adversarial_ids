\subsection{Defensive Countermeasures \& Model Hardening}
\label{sec:defense}

Having demonstrated the acute vulnerability of standard tree ensembles to Flow-Field Evasion, this section explores the dual defensive perspective. We formulate two complementary defense mechanisms: (i) Malleability-Penalized Tree Splitting (MPS) and (ii) Lie-Guided Adversarial Training (LGAT), and evaluate their defensive efficacy against adaptive flow steering.

\subsubsection{Vulnerability Root Cause: Feature Malleability Asymmetry}
Standard GBDT training algorithms (such as XGBoost's exact greedy split finder or LightGBM's histogram binning) evaluate split candidates $s = (j, \theta)$ purely on empirical training loss reduction:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \mathcal{L}(j, \theta) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{G_L^2}{H_L + \lambda} + \frac{G_R^2}{H_R + \lambda} - \frac{(G_L + G_R)^2}{H_L + H_R + \lambda} \right] - \gamma
\end{equation}
where $G_L, H_L$ and $G_R, H_R$ represent the first- and second-order gradient statistics of the left and right child subsets.

In network telemetry, features exhibit a severe \textbf{malleability asymmetry}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High-Malleability Features:} Metrics such as Flow Duration ($x_6$), Flow Bytes/s ($x_8$), and Flow Packets/s ($x_9$) have large Lie generator magnitudes ($\|\vec{v}_1(x)\| \gg 0$) and can be shifted arbitrarily by delaying packets.
    \item \textbf{Low-Malleability Features:} Attributes such as Destination Port ($x_0$), Protocol ($x_1$), and Server Response Bytes ($x_5$) have zero generator magnitudes ($\|\vec{v}_k(x)_i\| = 0$) and cannot be modified by the attacker.
\end{itemize}
Because high-malleability features exhibit wide statistical separation in synthetic testbeds, boosting algorithms greedily place them at the top root nodes of decision trees, creating widespread evasion vulnerability.

\subsubsection{Defense 1: Malleability-Penalized Tree Splitting (MPS)}
To force the ensemble to learn robust, invariant representations, we introduce Malleability-Penalized Tree Splitting. Expanding on robust tree split formulation principles established by Chen et al.~\cite{chen2019robust} and Calzavara et al.~\cite{calzavara2020treant}, the impurity gain score for splitting along feature $j$ during tree construction is regularized by its maximum physical Lie generator norm:

\begin{definition}[Feature Physical Malleability Index]
The physical malleability index $M_{\text{mal}}(j)$ of feature $j$ is defined as the empirical expectation over the training distribution of the maximum dimensionless variation induced by the Lie Cone $\mathcal{K}_{\text{net}}$:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\text{mal}}(j) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}} \left[ \max_{k \in \{1, \dots, 4\}} \frac{\delta s_k |\vec{v}_k(x)_j|}{\sigma_j} \right]
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_j$ is the empirical standard deviation of feature $j$ across the training corpus $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$, and $\delta s_k$ denotes the characteristic single-step perturbation scale for generator $k$ ($\delta s_1 = 10^4\,\mu\text{s}, \delta s_2 = 100\,\text{B}, \delta s_3 = 1\,\text{pkt}, \delta s_4 = 10^3\,\text{B}$).
\end{definition}

The robust split selection objective becomes:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Gain}_{\text{robust}}(j, \theta) = \Delta \mathcal{L}(j, \theta) - \alpha_{\text{mal}} \cdot \overline{\Delta \mathcal{L}} \cdot M_{\text{mal}}(j)
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\Delta \mathcal{L}}$ is the baseline mean impurity gain across candidate splits at the node, ensuring dimensional consistency, and $\alpha_{\text{mal}} \ge 0$ is a dimensionless hyperparameter governing the trade-off between clean training loss and adversarial malleability robustness. In production GBDTs, this objective can be implemented via custom split finding routines or simulated via feature-subsampling weights (\texttt{colsample\_bytree}) inversely proportional to $M_{\text{mal}}(j)$.

\paragraph{Theoretical Impact of MPS.}
When $\alpha_{\text{mal}} > 0$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Splits along Duration ($x_6$) and derived rate features ($x_8, x_9$) incur substantial penalties due to high dimensional responsiveness ($M_{\text{mal}}(j) \gg 0$).
    \item Splits along immutable protocol headers (Destination Port $x_0$, Protocol $x_1$) incur strictly zero penalty ($M_{\text{mal}}(j) = 0$).
    \item The boosting algorithm is biased toward decision paths dominated by non-malleable protocol attributes. Consequently, an attacker attempting evasion via temporal dilation alone cannot easily flip the tree leaf assignments.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Defense 2: Lie-Guided Adversarial Training (LGAT)}
While MPS regularizes the tree topology, model robustness can be further hardened through adversarial training. Standard adversarial training formulations (e.g., Madry et al. \cite{madry2018towards}) assume an $L_p$ perturbation ball $\mathcal{B}_\epsilon(x) = \{x + \delta \mid \|\delta\|_p \le \epsilon\}$. In network security, this generates physically invalid training samples that degrade clean accuracy \cite{hashemi2020enhancing, apruzzese2022modeling}.

We formulate \textbf{Lie-Guided Adversarial Training (LGAT)} as a saddle-point min-max game restricted to the physical flow manifold:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\Theta} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \max_{u \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{budget}}} \mathcal{L}\left( F_\Theta\left( \mathcal{A}_{\text{cone}}\left( x_i, u \right) \right), y_i \right)
\end{equation}
where the inner maximization searches along the integral curves of the Lie generators:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{A}_{\text{cone}}(x_i, u) = x_i + \int_0^{T_{\text{max}}} \sum_{k=1}^4 u_k(\tau) \vec{v}_k(x(\tau)) d\tau \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{flow}}
\end{equation}
and the control budget is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{U}_{\text{budget}} = \left\{ u \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^4 \;\middle|\; \sum_{k=1}^4 c_k \int_0^{T_{\text{max}}} u_k(\tau) d\tau \le \Gamma \right\}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Algorithmic Realization of LGAT.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:lgat} outlines the adversarial training loop for tree ensembles. At round $t=1$, the base tree is initialized on clean data. At each subsequent boosting iteration $t \ge 2$, the training batch is augmented with adversarial flows synthesized by running FFE for step budget $M_{\text{train}} = 5$ against the active ensemble $F_{t-1}$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Lie-Guided Adversarial Training (LGAT) for Tree Ensembles}
\label{alg:lgat}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Training set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^N$, number of boosting rounds $T$, adversarial ratio $\beta = 0.5$, step budget $M_{\text{train}} = 5$
\ENSURE Robust tree ensemble $F_T(x)$
\STATE Initialize base tree $F_0 \leftarrow \text{FitCleanBaseTree}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}})$
\FOR{$t = 1$ \TO $T$}
    \STATE $\mathcal{D}_{\text{aug}} \leftarrow \emptyset$
    \FOR{\textbf{each } $(x_i, y_i) \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$}
        \IF{$y_i = 1$ \AND $\text{Uniform}(0, 1) < \beta$}
            \STATE $x_{\text{adv}} \leftarrow \text{FFE}(F_{t-1}, x_i, \text{budget}=M_{\text{train}})$
            \STATE Add $(x_{\text{adv}}, 1)$ to $\mathcal{D}_{\text{aug}}$
        \ELSE
            \STATE Add $(x_i, y_i)$ to $\mathcal{D}_{\text{aug}}$
        \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Compute negative gradients $g_i$ and Hessians $h_i$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{aug}}$
    \STATE Fit tree $f_t$ using robust split scoring $\text{Gain}_{\text{robust}}$
    \STATE $F_t(x) \leftarrow F_{t-1}(x) + \eta \cdot f_t(x)$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \RETURN $F_T$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Empirical Defense Validation}
We evaluated one undefended baseline and three defensively hardened XGBoost models on CSE-CIC-IDS2018:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Standard XGBoost (Undefended):} Baseline histogram booster.
    \item \textbf{MPS-Hardened XGBoost:} Trained with Malleability Penalty $\alpha_{\text{mal}} = 0.5$.
    \item \textbf{LGAT-Hardened XGBoost:} Adversarially trained with $50\%$ Lie trajectory augmentation ($\beta = 0.5$).
    \item \textbf{Dual-Hardened (MPS + LGAT):} Both defenses enabled simultaneously.
\end{enumerate}

Figure~\ref{fig:defense_evaluation} and Table~\ref{tab:defense_evaluation} report the clean test accuracy and robust adversarial resistance under active FFE attack.

\paragraph{Analysis of Defensive Hardening.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mitigation of Flow-Field Evasion:} Combining Malleability-Penalized Splitting with Lie-Guided Adversarial Training substantially suppresses adaptive evasion, reducing the Attack Success Rate of FFE from \textbf{95.0\% down to 56.0\%} and boosting robust true-positive detection under adaptive physical Lie perturbation from \textbf{5.0\% to 44.0\%}.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Clean Accuracy Trade-Off:} Dual hardening incurs a modest clean accuracy reduction of $2.42$ percentage points ($99.72\% \to 97.30\%$), preserving a clean ROC-AUC of \textbf{99.69\%}. This demonstrates that penalizing easily malleable duration and rate features forces the tree ensemble to rely on structural protocol attributes, establishing a grounded defense against adaptive problem-space evasion.
\end{enumerate}
