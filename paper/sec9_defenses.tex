\section{Defensive Countermeasures}
\label{sec:defense}

Standard boosting chooses split features by training-loss reduction alone, so features that separate the classes widely in benchmark corpora, such as duration and the rates, sit near the root although an attacker can shift them by delaying or padding packets. We evaluate malleability-penalized splitting (MPS), which discourages splits on malleable features, and Lie-guided adversarial training (LGAT), which trains on flows generated along the Lie generators.

\subsection{Malleability-penalized tree splitting}

Following robust split formulations~\cite{chen2019robust, calzavara2020treant}, the split score for feature $j$ is regularized by its physical malleability index $M_{\text{mal}}(j)$:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\text{mal}}(j) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}} \left[ \max_{k \in \{1, \dots, 4\}} \frac{\delta s_k |\vec{v}_k(x)_j|}{\sigma_j} \right]
\end{equation}
The robust split selection objective is
\begin{equation}
    \text{Gain}_{\text{robust}}(j, \theta) = \Delta \mathcal{L}(j, \theta) - \alpha_{\text{mal}} \cdot \overline{\Delta \mathcal{L}} \cdot M_{\text{mal}}(j)
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_j$ is the feature standard deviation, $\delta s_k$ is the perturbation scale ($\delta s_1 = 10^4\,\mu\text{s}, \delta s_2 = 100\,\text{B}, \delta s_3 = 1\,\text{pkt}, \delta s_4 = 10^3\,\text{B}$), $\Delta \mathcal{L}(j, \theta)$ is the impurity gain, $\overline{\Delta \mathcal{L}}$ is the baseline mean gain, and $\alpha_{\text{mal}} \ge 0$ balances clean loss against malleability. The experiments evaluate the $\alpha_{\text{mal}} \to \infty$ limit by masking duration ($x_6$) and rates ($x_8, x_9$) to zero in training, test, and attack inputs.

\subsection{Lie-guided adversarial training}

Standard adversarial training assumes an $L_p$ ball around each input~\cite{madry2018towards}, which in network security generates physically invalid telemetry that degrades clean accuracy~\cite{hashemi2020enhancing, apruzzese2022modeling}. LGAT instead restricts the inner maximization to the physical flow manifold:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\Theta} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \max_{u \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{budget}}} \mathcal{L}\left( F_\Theta\left( \mathcal{A}_{\text{cone}}\left( x_i, u \right) \right), y_i \right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}_{\text{cone}}(x, u)$ is the flow reached along Lie generator trajectories under control budget $\mathcal{U}_{\text{budget}}$. In our implementation (Supplementary Algorithm~S4), FFE adversarial examples ($M=5$) are generated against the undefended model for 50\% of malicious training flows and appended to the training set.

\subsection{Evaluation}

We compared an undefended XGBoost model with three hardened variants on real CSE-CIC-IDS2018 flow traffic, each with 60 trees of depth 6 and learning rate 0.1. All models are evaluated on the cohort of $N=95$ detected malicious flows, using FFE at step budget $M=5$ (Table~\ref{tab:defense_evaluation}).

Combining MPS with LGAT dramatically suppresses FFE evasion from 51.6\% to 5.3\% and raises robust recall from 48.4\% to 94.7\%, with virtually zero degradation in clean classification performance (98.67\% accuracy and 99.84\% ROC-AUC, compared to 98.62\% and 99.88\% undefended).

\paragraph{The Malleability Penalty Trap: Why Standalone MPS Backfires}
A striking empirical phenomenon in Table~\ref{tab:defense_evaluation} is that standalone MPS severely degrades model robustness: FFE evasion surges from 51.6\% on the undefended model to 98.9\% on the MPS-hardened model (robust recall collapses to 1.1\%). This counter-intuitive vulnerability arises from what we term the \textit{malleability penalty trap}. When split gain penalties heavily suppress malleable temporal features (flow duration $x_6$, flow bytes/s $x_8$, and flow packets/s $x_9$), standard greedy tree induction must find alternative partitions to minimize empirical training loss. The ensemble is consequently forced to place its primary decision splits on unpenalized features, notably forward packet counts ($x_2$), forward payload bytes ($x_4$), transport header length ($x_{11}$), and TCP window size ($x_7$). 

Crucially, because these alternative features are not regularized by adversarial examples, the resulting decision boundaries sit immediately adjacent to natural attack clusters. For an adversary equipped with FFE, this boundary migration drastically simplifies the evasion path: instead of having to expend substantial query budgets diluting coupled rate equations via temporal pacing ($\vec{v}_{\text{time}}$), the attacker can execute instantaneous boundary snaps (TSBS) using infinitesimal chaff packet injection ($\vec{v}_{\text{chaff}}$), payload padding ($\vec{v}_{\text{pad}}$), or TCP window adjustment ($\vec{v}_{\text{win}}$) with near-zero cost in only 1--3 queries. Standalone feature penalization therefore creates an acute blind spot by shifting splits onto unfortified mutable dimensions. 

In contrast, Lie-guided adversarial training (LGAT) exposes the trees to valid Lie cone trajectories during induction, halving evasion to 25.3\%. When integrated in the dual-hardened configuration, MPS successfully prevents the ensemble from learning spurious shortcuts on rate and timing telemetry, while LGAT simultaneously inoculates the remaining discrete feature splits against Lie cone boundary traversal. This synergy demonstrates that problem-space adversarial training cannot rely on heuristic feature masking alone, but requires manifold-aware data augmentation to achieve certified robustness without sacrificing clean detection fidelity.
