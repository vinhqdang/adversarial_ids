\section{Defensive Countermeasures}
\label{sec:defense}

Standard boosting picks split features by loss reduction alone, so features that separate the classes in benchmark corpora, such as duration and the rates, can sit near the root although an attacker can shift them by delaying or padding packets. We evaluate malleability-penalized splitting (MPS), which discourages splits on malleable features, and Lie-guided adversarial training (LGAT), which trains on flows generated along the Lie generators.

\subsection{Malleability-penalized tree splitting}

Following robust split formulations~\cite{chen2019robust, calzavara2020treant}, the split score for feature $j$ is regularized by its physical malleability index $M_{\text{mal}}(j)$:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\text{mal}}(j) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}} \left[ \max_{k \in \{1, \dots, 4\}} \frac{\delta s_k |\vec{v}_k(x)_j|}{\sigma_j} \right]
\end{equation}
The robust split selection objective is
\begin{equation}
    \text{Gain}_{\text{robust}}(j, \theta) = \Delta \mathcal{L}(j, \theta) - \alpha_{\text{mal}} \cdot \overline{\Delta \mathcal{L}} \cdot M_{\text{mal}}(j)
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_j$ is the feature standard deviation, $\delta s_k$ is the perturbation scale ($\delta s_1 = 10^4\,\mu\text{s}, \delta s_2 = 100\,\text{B}, \delta s_3 = 1\,\text{pkt}, \delta s_4 = 10^3\,\text{B}$), $\Delta \mathcal{L}(j, \theta)$ is the impurity gain, $\overline{\Delta \mathcal{L}}$ is the baseline mean gain, and $\alpha_{\text{mal}} \ge 0$ balances clean loss against malleability. The experiments evaluate the $\alpha_{\text{mal}} \to \infty$ limit by masking duration ($x_6$) and rates ($x_8, x_9$) to zero in training, test, and attack inputs.

\subsection{Lie-guided adversarial training}

Standard adversarial training assumes an $L_p$ ball around each input~\cite{madry2018towards}, which in network security generates physically invalid telemetry that degrades clean accuracy~\cite{hashemi2020enhancing, apruzzese2022modeling}. LGAT instead restricts the inner maximization to the physical flow manifold:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\Theta} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \max_{u \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{budget}}} \mathcal{L}\left( F_\Theta\left( \mathcal{A}_{\text{cone}}\left( x_i, u \right) \right), y_i \right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}_{\text{cone}}(x, u)$ is the flow reached along Lie generator trajectories under control budget $\mathcal{U}_{\text{budget}}$. In our implementation (Supplementary Algorithm~S4), FFE adversarial examples ($M=5$) are generated against the undefended model for a random sample of 5,000 malicious training flows (about 1.3\% of the 381,000 malicious flows of the training partition, capped to keep the generation cost bounded) and appended to the training set.

\subsection{Evaluation}

We compared an undefended XGBoost model with three hardened variants on the full CSE-CIC-IDS2018 capture, each with 60 trees of depth 6 and learning rate 0.1, trained on the whole training partition (3.0 million flows). All models are evaluated on the balanced cohort of $N=300$ malicious flows that the undefended model detects, using FFE at step budget $M=5$ (Table~\ref{tab:defense_evaluation}). All three defenses leave clean detection unchanged (accuracy 98.04\% for every model, ROC-AUC 98.00\%--98.07\%), so none of the robustness comes at a visible cost in clean performance on this corpus. The undefended model is evaded in 96.3\% of the cohort at $M=5$. Masking the duration and rate features (MPS) lowers this to 67.0\%, Lie-guided adversarial training (LGAT) to 33.0\%, and the combination to 38.0\%. LGAT alone is therefore the strongest of the three, and combining it with MPS does not help: the 5-point difference between LGAT (33.0\%) and MPS+LGAT (38.0\%) is within the sampling error of a 300-flow cohort (Wilson 95\% intervals of roughly $\pm5$ points) and we make no claim that one is better than the other.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} MPS removes the three features an attacker moves most cheaply (duration and the two rates) and evasion falls by 29 points, but the ensemble still splits on forward packet counts, payload bytes, header length and window size, which FFE moves with small chaff, padding and window changes. LGAT shows the model flows reached along the same generators and brings evasion down by 63 points even though only 1.3\% of the malicious training flows were augmented. Residual evasion of 33\% against an attacker who uses exactly the generators the model was trained on shows that small-budget augmentation is not a certificate of robustness. We evaluated only the attack that generated the augmentation, so the figures do not measure robustness to a different attack such as the CAPGD baseline; the defenses were also evaluated on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 only, where FFE is saturated against the undefended model.
