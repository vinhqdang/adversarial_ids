\section*{S3. Diagnosis of FFE-v1 and validation tuning of FFE-v2}

All diagnostics and all tuning used cohorts of up to 1,000 detected malicious flows drawn from the 10\% validation split; nothing on the test cohorts was used (\texttt{results/ffev2\_diagnosis.json}, \texttt{results/ffev2\_tuning.json}; the numbers below that are not in these files are from \texttt{results/ffev2\_report.md}, which is generated from them). On these cohorts FFE-v1 reached 99.7/99.7/58.3\% (XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost) on CSE-CIC-IDS2018, 99.2/35.1/34.2\% on CIC-IDS2017 and 60.9/64.4/72.8\% on UNSW-NB15.

\textbf{UNSW-NB15.} On the 391 flows where FFE-v1 fails against XGBoost, the projected control evades 326. Its successful moves raise the forward packet count (99\%, median +213 packets), 82\% of them without changing the byte total (the projection fills the header sum at 20~B per extra packet), 7\% change the duration and none the window (every TCP flow in UNSW-NB15 advertises 0 or 255~B). FFE-v1 cannot make this move: chaff packets add 28--52~B of payload each, which pulls the forward mean towards 40~B instead of down to 3--5~B, the chaff step is capped at 50\% of the packet count per iteration, padding raises the mean and dilation only lowers the rates. The score then plateaus, since several splits on the packet count, the mean length and the packet rate must be crossed jointly, and FFE-v1 spends its whole budget (89--94 queries). A random multi-start reachability test (20,000 joint draws over delay, padding, chaff, segmentation and window per failed flow, 80 failed flows per cell) finds a boundary crossing for 100\% of the failed XGBoost and LightGBM flows and 97.5\% of the CatBoost ones, so these failures are algorithmic, not structural.

\textbf{CIC-IDS2017.} FFE-v1's window target 64,240~B is almost out of support (0.08\% of benign and 0.015\% of attack TCP flows; 11.5\% of benign flows advertise 65,535~B). For XGBoost the 8 flows FFE-v1 misses are all fixed by a window move. For LightGBM 90\% of the sampled failed flows are reachable (the unreachable ones are DoS flows) and the control wins only 17 of 649 failures, all through window moves. For CatBoost only 3 of 80 sampled failed flows (3.75\%) are reachable, none of the 37 DDoS and 40 DoS flows (the three are port scans): the immutable-feature ceiling of the main text; random search cannot prove unreachability. CatBoost's 22 window borders stop at 32,851.5, so 45,000, 64,240 and 65,535 share one bin and the window generator is inert for it there.

\textbf{CSE-CIC-IDS2018 CatBoost.} FFE-v1 evades 58.3\%; the control evades 411 of the 414 failures with a pure window move. The single target 64,240~B lies on the wrong side of CatBoost's borders, whereas the in-support benign values (8,192; 65,535; 219; 237; 279; 256) do not.

\textbf{Where FFE-v1 stalls.} (i) One hard-coded window target. (ii) One fixed magnitude per generator and iteration, too small to cross plateaus or too large for cheap flows. (iii) No generator that lowers the forward mean or raises the packet count without adding payload. (iv) Progress measured on the saturating probability, where float32 probabilities near 1 give tied probes. (v) The unit cost applied twice in the generator ranking.

\section*{S6. Defense details}

\textbf{Recipe.} The gate is trained on the schema columns without the window, the two rates and the mean length, with monotone constraints $+1$ on forward packets, forward bytes, duration and header bytes and larger capacity (XGBoost 300 trees of depth 8; LightGBM 300 trees of depth 8 with 200 leaves; CatBoost 300 iterations of depth 8). The orbit set has 100,000 transformed rows; adversarial training uses two rounds of 3,000 crafted flows, alternating (FFE-v1 with $M=5$ and $M=20$) and (FFE-v1 with $M=20$ and the white-box attacker); LGAT uses 5,000 FFE-v1 flows. The monotone-safe ratio features (backward/forward ratios) gave no gain in exploration and are not used. A manipulation-signature detector is implemented (\texttt{src/defenses/anomaly.py}) but was not evaluated, and random forests are supported by the recipe (the certificate test passes for them) but were not run. In exploration on validation, raising XGBoost's capacity from 60$\times$6 to 300$\times$8 lifted the monotone model's accuracy from 84.9\% to 86.4\% on UNSW-NB15 and from 99.85\% to 99.94\% on CIC-IDS2017; the follow-up below separates capacity from the constraint with an unconstrained 300$\times$8 arm.

\textbf{Follow-up checks} (\texttt{results/defense\_v2\_followup.md}; models were retrained with the same seeds and reproduce the saved cohorts and clean accuracy exactly; Tables~S12 and~S13). \emph{Whole-cohort evasion} is the share of the 1,000-flow cohort that is not flagged after the attack, counting flows the model already misses as evaded; for the undefended models it equals Real ASR. (i) \emph{Hiding the backward columns.} The gate G35 additionally hides $x_3$ and $x_5$, which would remove the assumption that backward traffic does not change. It is costly: accuracy and F1 fall by about 4 and 19 points on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and by 4.7--5.2 and about 9 points on CIC-IDS2017 relative to the undefended model, and by 12--14 accuracy points on UNSW-NB15; its benign false-positive rate is 2.1\%, 5.9--6.1\% and 36--37\%, and it flags only 74--76\% of the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 cohort. \section*{S7. White-box leaf-tuple-style control}

The official LT-Attack code needs Bazel and a C++ compiler, which this machine lacks, so we wrote a greedy attack in the same spirit (\texttt{src/attacks/whitebox\_tree.py}). For XGBoost it reads all 60 trees; each step moves one feature to the other side of one split threshold, choosing the move that lowers the exact ensemble margin most, for at most 20 steps (margins agree with \texttt{predict\_proba} to $2\times10^{-7}$). It is not LT-Attack's leaf-tuple search with an $L_\infty$ objective. Two variants share the projection and bookkeeping of the other attacks: \emph{project at end} (unconstrained attack, then projection, then re-scoring) and \emph{project in loop} (every candidate is projected before scoring). Both use all 1,000 cohort flows, two target queries (the tree reads are not counted; about 300--1,000 internal margin evaluations per flow) and are schema-feasible by construction. For LightGBM and CatBoost the control of Table~2 is our generic version on the raw ensemble margin (\texttt{RawMarginModel}), run with projection in the loop; its 56.8\% on UNSW-NB15 CatBoost may reflect single-feature greedy steps stalling on oblivious trees, which we did not test.

\section*{S8. Packet-level replay}

We built a synthetic replay (\texttt{src/validation/packet\_replay.py}); no packet capture was used and nothing was sent over a network. A flow record is expanded into a packet sequence with the stated packet counts, payload and header totals, duration and initial window, and a CICFlowMeter-style extractor (our re-implementation, not CICFlowMeter) recomputes the twelve features. For 20,000 flows per corpus the round trip is exact for every flow on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CIC-IDS2017 and for 19,999 of 20,000 on UNSW-NB15 (one one-packet record with a duration of 1~$\mu$s); this shows that the schema is jointly realizable by packet lists, not that it matches a real capture.

For FFE-v2 the integer action ledger of each successful flow (delay, chaff, re-segmented packets, padding, window) is applied to the trace; segmentation halves the largest forward packet $K$ times, and tests confirm that payload, duration, first forward packet and window are unchanged. The generators book 28--52~B of forward payload per chaff packet, whereas a minimal packet carries none, so three conventions are replayed: \emph{schema} (chaff carries the booked bytes), \emph{empty chaff, bytes as padding} (empty chaff, same feature vector), and \emph{empty chaff, bytes dropped} (the booked bytes are not delivered). Table~S15 gives the share of schema-level evasions that remain evasive (XGBoost, first 300 flows per corpus). Under the first two conventions 100\% of replayed flows reproduce the schema vector within 1\% on all twelve features and stay feasible, with no unmet segmentation or padding. The header-length mismatch of FFE-v1 does not arise because the ledger, not the vector difference, drives the replay.

\section*{S9. Why LightGBM resisted FFE-v1 on CIC-IDS2017, and the fix}

On the 1,000-flow cohort FFE-v1 evaded XGBoost in 99.2\% and LightGBM in 35.1\%. By family, LightGBM was evaded in 100\% of port scans, botnet and brute-force flows but in 1.9\% of DoS and 0.0\% of DDoS flows, about 66\% of the cohort; XGBoost was evaded in 98.1\% and 100\%. Threshold extraction and the probe are correct for LightGBM, start scores are identical (median 0.998) and the gain profiles are nearly the same (backward payload 0.31 for both, duration 0.29 for both), so reliance on immutable features does not explain the gap. Setting only the initial window to 64,240~B, FFE-v1's target, flips 97.2\% of the 33,857 detected DoS test flows for XGBoost and 0.9\% for LightGBM (99.96\% against 0.0\% for DDoS). Almost no training flow has a window between 29,200 and 65,535~B (64,240~B itself occurs in 0.08\% of benign and 0.015\% of attack TCP flows; \texttt{results/diagnosis\_lightgbm.md}); XGBoost splits at 32,831 and 65,535, so 64,240~B lands on the benign side, whereas LightGBM splits at 63,586 and 65,435 and keeps it on the malicious side. More leaves, finer histogram bins and a smaller minimum leaf size change this by about one point. Moving only the window target to the in-support value 65,535~B lifted FFE-v1 on LightGBM from 35.1\% to 93.7\% (queries 120 to 21.8) and left XGBoost at 99.2\%. FFE-v2 replaces the single target by the attacker's most frequent benign windows and reaches 96.0\% on LightGBM with 32.4 queries; the residual failures are concentrated in DoS flows (90.5\% evaded) and were not examined further.
