\section*{S4. Ablations}

The ablations use the CUDA XGBoost target of CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and a balanced cohort of $N=300$ detected malicious flows (up to 50 per attack family). Table~S2 reports three sweeps: Panel A varies the step budget $M$, Panel B isolates generator subsets, and Panel C compares operational cost regimes. The TSBS relative snapping offset $\epsilon_{\text{rel}}$ was swept over $\{0, 10^{-6}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-2}, 1\}$.

\textbf{Step budget.} ASR rises from 52.7\% at $M=1$ (5.0 queries) through 89.7\% at $M=3$ and 96.3\% at $M=5$ to 100.0\% at $M=20$ (10.3 queries) and is flat beyond. A single step already evades more than half of the cohort, which is why the mean query count stays near ten. Physical feasibility is 100.0\% for every budget.

\textbf{Generators.} No single generator suffices. Temporal dilation alone evades 57.0\% of the flows but needs 33.5 queries and adds 39.8\,s of flow duration on average; window morphing alone evades 53.0\% with 2.6 queries and no added delay; chaff alone 47.7\%; padding alone 30.0\%. Pairs are better than singles (time plus chaff 82.7\%, time plus padding 68.0\%, padding plus chaff 49.0\% with no added delay), and the complete cone reaches 100.0\% with 10.3 queries and 0.22\,s of mean delay. The full cone needs fewer queries than the time-based subsets because the cheap window and chaff moves are available when delay alone would stall.

\textbf{Cost regimes and snapping offset.} These do not discriminate on this corpus. All four cost regimes reach 100.0\% ASR with 9.8--13.9 queries, and the snapping offset leaves ASR at 100.0\% and the queries between 10.2 and 10.3. The regimes do change which generator dominates (time under the balanced regime, window morphing under the latency-sensitive regime) and the mean added delay (216\,ms under regime A against 91\,ms under regime B), so FFE responds to the operator's cost model, but because the corpus is saturated we have no evidence on how the regime affects success; a harder target such as UNSW-NB15 would be needed for that.

\section*{S5. Cross-architecture transferability}

Flows crafted by FFE against a source ensemble were scored by three independent target architectures ($N=300$ detected CSE-CIC-IDS2018 flows; Table~S3). Flows crafted against XGBoost evade LightGBM in 69.0\% of the cases, Random Forest in 81.3\% and CatBoost in 37.7\%. LightGBM flows evade XGBoost in 82.3\% and Random Forest in 83.0\%, CatBoost flows evade LightGBM in 66.7\%, and Random Forest flows evade XGBoost in 79.0\%. CatBoost is the hardest target for every source (34.0\%--37.7\%); this is consistent with its symmetric oblivious trees placing thresholds differently from the leaf-wise and depth-wise ensembles, although we did not test that explanation. Attacks against CatBoost itself succeed in 99.3\% of the cases, so its resistance to transfer reflects dissimilar boundaries and not a stronger model. Excluding CatBoost as target, 54\%--83\% of the transferred flows evade.
